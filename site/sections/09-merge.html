Exposé VI

Extensions and merging

Adapters, prompts and ReFT enlarge the network before they train, so merging them back is a lifting problem. Seven local rewrite rules give sufficient conditions for a merge and name the side condition that fails when they stop.

When a lift fails, look for the place where it fails, and give the obstruction a name.

after Grothendieck
In one minute
  • Adapters, prefixes, prompts and ReFT add boxes to the network. Merging one back means finding a weight of the original network that computes the trained function.
  • Seven rewrite rules slide an edit into a weight that absorbs it. They certify a merge when the edit acts alike at every position, the absorbing weights are unshared, shifts have bias slots and, under RoPE, scales are constant on rotary pairs. A stuck rewrite shows only a box-local obstruction.
  • (IA)³’s feed-forward scale always folds into \(W_{\rm down}\). It folds into \(W_{\rm up}\) under SwiGLU for every scale and under ReLU for nonnegative ones, and under GELU only entries \(0\) and \(1\) fold.
  • A prefix is a gated parallel adapter whose gate depends on the input, so for generic frozen weights it cannot be folded into the head’s own weights. Per hidden vector LoReFT is LoRA on an inserted identity box, and since it acts at selected positions no merge is known.

The weight that absorbs the edit

(IA)³ rescales the hidden units of a feed-forward block by a learned vector \(\ell\), which one would like to fold into a frozen weight after training. The weight that reads the hidden units, \(W_{\rm down}\), absorbs \(\ell\) under every activation. The weight that produces them, \(W_{\rm up}\), absorbs it under SwiGLU for every \(\ell\), under ReLU when no entry is negative, and under GELU only for entries \(0\) or \(1\).

The activation belongs to the network, which a weight-space method never sees (Remark I.3). Adapters, prefixes, prompts and ReFT add boxes the pretrained network lacked, and whether they can be removed depends on their surroundings. Figure 12 runs the rules of this exposé on one block.

Extensions, and merging as a lift

An adapter \(y\mapsto y+U\sigma(Dy)\) adds weights \((U,D)\), and at \(U=0\) the network computes the old function. A prefix adds key–value pairs to every softmax, and ReFT edits hidden states at chosen positions. Each time \(\Theta\) grows to \(\Theta\times\Theta'\) and \(f\) to a larger \(\tilde f\). A prefix always takes softmax mass, and on a head with \(W_oW_v\ne0\) no prefix leaves the output unchanged, so prefixes have no neutral setting there (Thm VI.5(f)).

Definition VI.1 (extensions; mergeability).
  • An extension is a function-preserving pointed embedding \(\iota:(\Theta,f,\theta_0)\to(\Theta\times\Theta',\tilde f,(\theta_0,\theta_0'))\), \(\iota(\theta)=(\theta,\theta_0')\), with \(\tilde f(\theta,\theta_0',-)=f(\theta,-)\). The point \(\theta_0'\) is the neutral point (“identity at initialisation”).
  • An unpointed extension has no neutral point.
  • A method on an extension is mergeable if there is \(\mu:Q\to\Theta\) with \(\Phi_{\tilde f}\circ\rho=\Phi_f\circ\mu\), a lift along the realisation.
  • It is box-locally mergeable if the modified box function stays in the box’s class: linear maps for a linear box, affine maps when the box has a bias slot. Theorem VI.3 gives sufficient conditions under which this yields a merge into the base network.

A merge is an arrow \(\mu\) that makes the square of realisations commute.

\[\begin{array}{ccc} Q & \hphantom{.}\xrightarrow{\qquad\mu\qquad}\hphantom{.} & \Theta\\[2pt] \llap{\scriptstyle\rho\ }\Big\downarrow & & \Big\downarrow\rlap{\scriptstyle\ \Phi_f}\\[2pt] \Theta\times\Theta' & \hphantom{.}\xrightarrow[\qquad\Phi_{\tilde f}\qquad]{}\hphantom{.} & C^\infty(X,Y) \end{array}\]

A weight-space method merges by \(\mu=\rho\), the canonical arrow to full fine-tuning (Obs I.4(a)), so the real questions live on extensions, in the slice of \(\mathbf{Para}\) over the pretrained model (Remark I.3). Box-local mergeability can be read off formulas, and the atlas of Exposé VIII records it as a merge type:

  • M1 the edit merges into its own box;
  • M1→ the rules move it into a neighbouring box, under side conditions;
  • Mq an exact merge exists but leaves the quantised type;
  • M∞ no merge: the rules are blocked box-locally, or the edit lies outside them.

“Zero inference latency” runs two questions together, whether the original architecture can compute the trained function at all and whether a local edit finds the weight. A zero up-projection, or the zero gate of LLaMA-Adapter, makes training start at the pretrained function, and by the function-level form of Lemma II.4 only \(U\), or the gate, moves at first.

The activation fixes the privileged basis

An edit moves through a network by sliding along wires, and a linear map slides through a box when it commutes with the box. Activations are the bottleneck. Permutations commute with every elementwise \(\sigma\), and for ReLU so do positive rescalings, since \(\mathrm{ReLU}(ct)=c\,\mathrm{ReLU}(t)\) for \(c\gt0\). The lemma says how little else does.

Lemma VI.2 (the activation fixes the privileged basis). Known Let \(\sigma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be an elementwise activation that is not affine, and let \(L\in GL_d\) commute with it as maps on \(\mathbb R^d\): \(L\,\sigma^{\times d}=\sigma^{\times d}L\).

(a) If \(\sigma\in C^2\), then \(L=PD\) is monomial.

(b) If \(\sigma\) is ReLU or leaky ReLU with slope \(\alpha\ne1\), then \(L=PD\) with \(D\) a positive diagonal.

(c) If moreover \(\sigma\) is real-analytic at \(0\) with \(\sigma(t)=\sum a_kt^k\), each diagonal entry \(c\) of \(D\) satisfies \(ca_k=c^ka_k\) for all \(k\). So \(c=1\) for GELU and SiLU, since \(\sigma''(0)\neq0\), and \(c=\pm1\) for tanh.

(expand)

(a) Fix row \(i\) of \(L\); its row vector \(\ell_i\) is nonzero because \(L\) is invertible. Coordinate \(i\) of \(L\,\sigma^{\times d}(x)\) is \(\sum_jL_{ij}\sigma(x_j)\), whose mixed partials \(\partial_j\partial_k\) vanish for \(j\ne k\). Coordinate \(i\) of \(\sigma^{\times d}(Lx)\) is \(\sigma(\ell_i\cdot x)\), with mixed partial \(L_{ij}L_{ik}\sigma''(\ell_i\cdot x)\). Since \(\sigma\) is not affine there is \(t_0\) with \(\sigma''(t_0)\ne0\); choose \(x\) with \(\ell_i\cdot x=t_0\). Then \(L_{ij}L_{ik}=0\) for \(j\ne k\), so each row has a single nonzero entry.

(b) ReLU is not \(C^2\), so argue by kinks instead. The left side of row \(i\) is non-differentiable exactly on \(\bigcup_{j:L_{ij}\ne0}\{x_j=0\}\), and the right side exactly on the hyperplane \(\{\ell_i\cdot x=0\}\). The two sets are equal, and a hyperplane equal to a finite union of coordinate hyperplanes is one of them. So row \(i\) has a single entry \(L_{ij}\), and \(L_{ij}\sigma(t)=\sigma(L_{ij}t)\) at \(t=1\) forces \(L_{ij}\gt0\). Leaky ReLU is identical.

(c) \(P\) commutes with \(\sigma^{\times d}\), so \(D\sigma=\sigma D\), that is \(c\,\sigma(t)=\sigma(ct)\) for each diagonal entry \(c\). Compare Taylor coefficients at \(0\). For GELU and SiLU \(a_2\ne0\), so \(c=c^2\) and \(c=1\). For tanh \(a_3\ne0\), so \(c=c^3\) and \(c=\pm1\). ∎

CreditThe lemma is classical. Sussmann (1992) and Chen, Lu and Hecht-Nielsen (1993) found the weight-space symmetries of tanh networks, and Godfrey et al. (NeurIPS 2022) computed the linear maps that commute with each common activation. Permutation symmetry is what Ainsworth et al., 2022 use to align whole models before merging them.

So rules R2 and R3 below exhaust the invertible linear maps that slide through \(\sigma\), permutations for GELU and SiLU and positive monomials for ReLU. In gated blocks such as SwiGLU every diagonal slides through the linear branch (rule R4), so their hidden-unit group is the full monomial group \(\mathrm{Mon}_{d_{\rm ff}}\).

More: symmetry groups by architecture

Ungated blocks (BERT, GPT-2, ViT) keep \(S_d\) under GELU and SiLU, \(B_d\) under tanh and the positive monomials under ReLU; gated blocks (LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma) keep \(\mathrm{Mon}_{d_{\rm ff}}\). LayerNorm’s privileged basis comes from its diagonal gain; a gain-free RMSNorm is \(O(d)\)-equivariant, which SliceGPT and QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2024) use to rotate the residual stream (Obs II.15). Prediction X.3 turns the lemma into a permutation test.

Seven local rules

Draw a transformer block as a string diagram, with a copy node where a wire feeds several boxes and a product node in a gated block. An edit is a small box on a wire. To merge, slide it by equalities of maps until a trainable box absorbs it; where it gets stuck names the failing side condition. A positional rotation, a shared weight, a missing bias slot or a second reader of a wire can each block a slide, and all four occur in production models.

Analogy (what “string diagram” and “spider” mean here). The diagrams are string diagrams of a symmetric monoidal category, read literally. Copy is the cartesian diagonal \(x\mapsto(x,x)\), which commutes with every map, and the product node is \(\odot\). “Spider” is only a name for both; they fail the Frobenius law, which is never used.

A trainable box is a node whose weight ranges over all linear maps, or over all affine maps when it has a bias slot. R1 is closure of these classes; R2–R7 are equalities of maps.

Consequences.

(a) Affine edits. Let a method insert a position-uniform affine edit \(E_q\) on a wire \(w\) of the base network \(f\), for every \(q\in Q\). If every reader of \(w\) is a trainable box of \(f\), tied to no other weight, whose class is closed under precomposition with \(E_q\) (a shift needs a bias slot), the method merges by \(W_c\mapsto W_cE_q\) for all readers \(c\) at once. Dually, it merges into an untied trainable box that writes \(w\) whenever \(E_q\) composed with that box stays in its class. R1–R7 do not merge position-selective edits (LoReFT, Activated LoRA) or edits on a wire also read by a skip connection, a norm or the output.

(b) Diagonal methods. A diagonal method merges into a box reached by R1–R7 if the rules move every copy of \(\mathrm{diag}(\ell)\) into trainable boxes for all \(\ell\) in the parameter domain (\(\ell\ge0\) when R3 is used); no position-dependent operator lies on the path (under RoPE, \(\ell\) is constant on rotary pairs); the target is unshared, or \(\ell\) is constant across the sharing group; and every shift has a bias slot. For generic frozen weights the RoPE condition is also necessary.

(c) Non-affine edits. For a linear or affine box, an edit whose modified box function is non-affine in the box input, for some parameter value, is not box-locally mergeable. For attention, see Thm VI.5(f).

(d) Type relativity. A generic high-precision update is off the quantisation grid, so an exact merge of a QLoRA or LoftQ update leaves the quantised type and a type-preserving merge is lossy. QA-LoRA’s group-constant adapters are the exception (Prop V.2(d)).

R1 is closure of linear (affine) maps under composition and sums. R2, R4, R6 and R7 are identities; R7 is the naturality of the cartesian diagonal. For R3, \(\ell\sigma(t)=\sigma(\ell t)\) for all \(\ell\ge0\) and all \(t\) is positive homogeneity of degree one; such a \(\sigma\) is linear on each half-line, and linear if it is \(C^1\). For R5, \(\langle R_mMq,R_nk\rangle=\langle q,M^\top R_{n-m}k\rangle\) for every \(M\), since \(R_m^\top R_n=R_{n-m}\), while \(\langle R_mq,R_nM^\top k\rangle=\langle q,R_{n-m}M^\top k\rangle\). The two agree for all \(q,k\) iff \(M^\top\) commutes with \(R_{n-m}\), and a diagonal matrix commutes with a planar rotation through an angle that is not a multiple of \(\pi\) iff its two entries are equal.

(a) The closure and reader conditions put \(W_cE_q\) in each reader’s class, R7 distributes \(E_q\) to every reader, and position-uniformity lets one weight absorb the edit at every position. The dual case is R1 applied to \(E_q\circ W\). (b) is (a) applied at the box reached. For the converse under RoPE, suppose a query scaling \(D=\mathrm{diag}(\ell)\) merges into some \(W_K'\). Equal logits give \(W_Q^\top R_\Delta W_K'=W_Q^\top DR_\Delta W_K\) for every offset \(\Delta\) that occurs. If \(W_Q\) and \(W_K\) have full row rank, then \(W_K'=R_{-\Delta}DR_\Delta W_K\) for every such \(\Delta\). Causal attention includes \(\Delta=0\), so \(D\) commutes with every occurring \(R_\Delta\), and \(\ell\) is constant on each rotary pair whose angle at some occurring \(\Delta\) is not a multiple of \(\pi\). (c) is the definition of box-local mergeability. (d) is Prop V.2(d).

For the (IA)³ corollary under GELU, suppose \(\ell_i\,\mathrm{GELU}(w_i\cdot x)=\mathrm{GELU}(w_i'\cdot x)\) for all \(x\), with \(w_i\ne0\). On the hyperplane \(w_i\cdot x=0\) the right side vanishes, and GELU vanishes only at \(0\), so \(w_i'=cw_i\). Then \(\ell_i\,\mathrm{GELU}(t)=\mathrm{GELU}(ct)\) for all \(t\), and the Taylor coefficients at \(0\), \(\mathrm{GELU}(t)=t/2+t^2/\sqrt{2\pi}+\dots\), give \(c=\ell_i\) and \(\ell_i=\ell_i^2\). Under ReLU an entry \(\ell_i\lt0\) is impossible, since the left side is then \(\le0\) and somewhere negative, while the right side is \(\ge0\). ∎

More: residual-stream edits that do merge

Some edits outside (a) merge anyway. A diagonal edit after a post-LN folds into that norm’s gain and shift; a shift after a pre-LN block with biases, into its FFN output bias; an orthogonal edit in an untied pre-RMSNorm model, globally, SliceGPT-style. No merge is known for LoReFT’s edit at interior positions.

Linear insertions, and ReFT

A linear inserted module fuses at once by R1, and the question is which weight-space method it becomes. ReFT, read one hidden vector at a time, becomes LoRA on a box the network lacks, the identity, written in homogeneous coordinates \((h,1)\) as \([I_d\mid0]\) so that its last column holds the bias.

Proposition VI.4 (linear insertions, and ReFT).

(a) A linear adapter in parallel with a single linear box, \(W_0x+sUDx\), is LoRA: it has the same \(\rho\).

(b) Let \(W_0\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) be bias-free. A serial linear adapter \((I+UD)W_0\), with \(U\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}\), \(D\in\mathbb R^{r\times m}\), \(r\ge1\) and the scale absorbed, has image \(W_0+\{Y\in\mathcal M_{\le r}:\mathrm{row}\,Y\subseteq\mathrm{row}\,W_0\}\). It is expressively equivalent to LoRA iff \(\operatorname{rank}W_0=n\), and isomorphic to LoRA, as unpointed methods, iff \(W_0\) is invertible, by \((U,D)\mapsto(U,DW_0)\). If \(r\lt m=n\) and LoRA is pointed at \((0,A_0)\) with \(\operatorname{rank}A_0=r\), a pointed isomorphism from the serial adapter at \((U_0,D_0)\) exists iff \(W_0\) is invertible and \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0=\mathrm{row}(D_0W_0)\); then \(U_0=0\).

(c) LoReFT is \(\Phi(h)=h+R^\top(Wh+b-Rh)\), with \(R\in\mathbb R^{r\times d}\) having orthonormal rows, applied at selected positions. On one hidden vector, \[\Phi(h)=\big(I+R^\top(W-R)\big)h+R^\top b,\] which is LoRA\(_r\) on the affine identity box \([I_d\mid0]\), with \(B=R^\top\) restricted to orthonormal columns and \(A=[W-R\mid b]\). Its image is \((I,0)+\mathcal M_{\le r}(d\times(d+1))\), of dimension \(r(2d+1-r)\). The bias is confined to \(\mathrm{col}\,B\), so for \(r\lt d\) this is smaller than LoRA\(_r\oplus\)BitFit, by \(d-r\). Its generic fibres are \(O(r)\)-orbits, \((R,W,b)\mapsto(OR,OW,Ob)\), so it is simulated by, and not isomorphic to, LoRA\(_r\) pointed at \((R_0^\top,0)\), a pointing with \(B_0\ne0\) and \(A_0=0\). It is pointed iff \(W=R\) and \(b=0\). The reference implementation, pyreft, initialises \(W\) and \(b\) by the default nn.Linear init, independently of \(R\), which leaves a pointing defect \(R^\top((W_0-R_0)h+b_0)\); at initialisation the \(R\)-component of \(h\) is replaced by a random affine function.

Conversely, an isomorphism preserves \(\mathrm{Im}\) (Obs I.4(c)), forcing \(\operatorname{rank}W_0=n\), and is a diffeomorphism, forcing \(2mr=r(m+n)\), that is \(m=n\). Pointed case, “if”: \(U_0D_0W_0=0\) and \(W_0\) invertible give \(U_0D_0=0\), and \(\operatorname{rank}(D_0W_0)=\operatorname{rank}A_0=r\) gives \(D_0\) full row rank, so \(U_0=0\). (c) Expand \(\Phi\). Every \(d\times(d+1)\) matrix of rank \(\le r\) factors as \(R^\top V\) with \(R^\top\) having orthonormal columns (QR), so the image is as stated. \(R^\top\) is injective, so the edit vanishes iff \(W=R\) and \(b=0\). Attention is a homomorphism followed by a perspective map

Fix a query \(q\). Attention over a context \(K\) of key–value pairs computes

\[\mathrm{Attn}_q(K)=\frac{\sum_{(k,v)\in K}e^{\langle q,k\rangle/\sqrt d}\,v}{\sum_{(k,v)\in K}e^{\langle q,k\rangle/\sqrt d}}.\]

The pair (total weight, weighted sum of values) is additive in the context, and only the division \((Z,S)\mapsto S/Z\), the perspective map of convex analysis, is nonlinear. With a prefix \(P\) before the content \(C\), the output averages what \(P\) alone and \(C\) alone would give, weighted by their shares of the mass, so the prefix is a parallel branch gated by its share.

Theorem VI.5 (attention is a homomorphism followed by a perspective map). Known in part Fix a query \(q\). Multisets of key–value pairs form a commutative monoid under \(\uplus\). Define
\[\Sigma_q(K)=\Big(\sum e^{\langle q,k\rangle/\sqrt d},\ \sum e^{\langle q,k\rangle/\sqrt d}\,v\Big).\]

(a) \(\Sigma_q\) is a monoid homomorphism into \((\mathbb R_{\ge0}\times\mathbb R^{d_v},+)\), landing in \(\{Z\gt0\}\cup\{(0,0)\}\). On nonempty multisets, \(\mathrm{Attn}_q=\pi\circ\Sigma_q\) with the partial map \(\pi(Z,S)=S/Z\), defined for \(Z\gt0\).

(b) Prefix is a gated parallel adapter. For nonempty \(P\) and \(C\), \(\mathrm{Attn}_q(P\uplus C)=(1-\lambda_q)\mathrm{Attn}_q(C)+\lambda_q\mathrm{Attn}_q(P)\), with \(\lambda_q=Z_q(P)/(Z_q(P)+Z_q(C))\).

(c) Per-layer pattern rigidity. Within one layer, for fixed \(q\) and \(C\), the content attention weights are multiplied by \(1-\lambda_q\), their ratios are unchanged, and the output lies on the segment from \(\mathrm{Attn}_q(C)\) to \(\mathrm{Attn}_q(P)\in\mathrm{conv}\,V_P\). Lower-layer prefixes change the queries, keys and values of higher layers, so attention patterns at the level of the network are not rigid.

(d) LLaMA-Adapter normalises \(P\) and \(C\) separately and adds a zero-initialised gate, \(\mathrm{Attn}_q(C)+g\,\mathrm{Attn}_q(P)\), with \(g=\tanh(g_l)\) per head in the paper and one raw scalar per layer in HF PEFT. It uses two perspective maps, leaves the content weights exactly unchanged, and is pointed at \(g=0\). Its prompt keys and values are the frozen \(W_k,W_v\) applied to a learned prompt in each adapted top layer.

(e) Prompt \(\to\) deep prefix in causal decoders. Assume that the soft prompt comes first, that attention is causal, and that content positions are offset by the prompt length in both methods (as HF PEFT does via cache_position). Then the states at prompt positions depend only on \(p\), and \(h:p\mapsto(K_j(p),V_j(p))_j\) satisfies \(\Phi\circ\rho_{\rm prefix}\circ h=\Phi\circ\rho_{\rm prompt}\) as functions of the content tokens, read at content positions. Without the offset, under RoPE, \(h\) must also rotate the prompt keys back by the prompt length. The methods live on different extensions, so this is an arrow of the unpointed slice over the realisation \(C^\infty(X_{\rm content},Y_{\rm content})\), not of \(\mathsf{Meth}\). It fails in bidirectional encoders of depth \(\ge2\).

(f) Obstructions. Call a head standard if it is vanilla softmax attention, without torch’s add_bias_kv or add_zero_attn, learned sinks or softmax-plus-one. On length-one contexts every weight setting of a standard head is affine in \(x\). For generic frozen head weights (in a bias-free head, \(W_o\ne0\) and \(W_q\ne0\) suffice) and generic prefixes, a prefixed head is not, because the gate \(\lambda(x)\) varies. So prefix tuning and P-tuning v2 are not box-locally mergeable. If \(W_oW_v\ne0\), no prefix is neutral for the head, so prompt and prefix tuning are generically unpointed.

(f) A softmax over one token equals \(1\), so the original head computes \(W_o(W_vx+b_v)+b_o\). The prefixed output is real-analytic in \((x,\text{prefix})\), and one explicit witness of a nonzero second derivative along a ray makes the prefixes with affine output a nowhere-dense analytic subset. If \(W_o=0\) the head is constant, and if \(W_q=0\) with \(W_k^\top b_q=0\) the gate is constant and the head stays affine, which is why genericity of the frozen weights is needed. For unpointedness, the prefixed output on one token is \(W_o\big[(1-\lambda(x))(W_vx+b_v)+\lambda(x)\,\mathrm{Attn}_{q(x)}(P)\big]+b_o\) with \(\lambda(x)\gt0\). It equals the frozen output iff \(W_o\mathrm{Attn}_{q(x)}(P)=W_o(W_vx+b_v)\) for all \(x\). The left side lies in the compact set \(W_o\,\mathrm{conv}\,V_P\), and the right side is unbounded when \(W_oW_v\ne0\). ∎
