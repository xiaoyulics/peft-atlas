Exposé III

Fibres and metrics

The image of a method fixes what it can reach. Its fibres and the optimizer’s metric fix how it gets there, so isomorphic methods can train differently and different-looking methods can train identically.

Before asking how a method learns, ask what it cannot help keeping.

after Grothendieck
In one minute
  • Training with rates \(\Lambda\) moves the weights by \(\dot\theta=-K\nabla_\theta L\), with the cometric \(K=D\rho\,\Lambda\,D\rho^\top\), which depends on where the parameters sit in their fibre.
  • A 2-torus of rescalings acts on LoRA’s knobs \((\alpha,\eta_A,\eta_B,\sigma_A)\) without changing training. Under Adam with \(\varepsilon=0\), no decay or clipping and one shared schedule, LoRA+ with ratio \(\lambda\) from \(B_0=0\) is plain LoRA with \(\alpha\) multiplied by \(\lambda\) (after Schulman et al., 2025).
  • Gradient flow conserves LoRA’s charge \(\Phi=B^\top B/\eta_B-AA^\top/\eta_A\). It sorts initialisations, and when it is isotropic one scale \(\sigma^*\) separates a LoRA-FA-like regime from balanced dynamics.
  • On full-rank factors, SGD respects \(O(r)\) of the gauge, Adam only the signed permutations. So from a zero-\(B\) start the image depends only on \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\), SGD’s trajectory on \(A_0^\top A_0\), and Adam’s on \(A_0\) up to signed row permutation.

One experiment first

Training happens upstairs, in the parameter space \(Q\), where each fibre of \(\rho\) collects the parameters that give one weight. The optimizer’s metric on \(Q\), pushed down to weight space, is what the model feels. Every statement below names its optimizer.

The metric a method carries

A learning rate is a metric in disguise. Gradient descent with rates \(\Lambda\) moves \(q\) by \(-\Lambda\nabla_q\), and pushed down by \(D\rho\) this is the weight gradient multiplied by a positive semidefinite operator.

Definition III.1 (trained method; cometric; three equivalences). A trained method is a method together with optimizer data: a learning-rate operator \(\Lambda\succ0\) (per-block rates), an initial scale, and an update rule. Gradient flow \(\dot q=-\Lambda\nabla_q(L\circ\rho)\) moves the weights by \[\dot\theta=-K_q\nabla_\theta L,\qquad K_q=D\rho_q\,\Lambda\,D\rho_q^\top.\] We call \(K_q\) the induced cometric. For a backward method with compression \(c_t\) and anchor \(a_t\) (Def IV.1), \(K_t=a_tc_t\).

Two methods are expressively equivalent if their images agree, first-order equivalent if their \(K_{q_0}\) agree, and dynamically equivalent if they produce the same \(\theta\)-trajectory for every loss.

Cometrics compose the way derivatives do.

Observation III.2 (chain rule for cometrics). Let \(\sigma:Q'\to Q\) and \(\rho:Q\to X\) be smooth, with \(X=\Theta\) or a function or output space. Train \(Q'\) by gradient flow with a constant rate operator \(\Lambda'\succ0\), and put \(K^\sigma_{q'}:=D\sigma_{q'}\Lambda'D\sigma_{q'}^\top\). Then \[K^{\rho\sigma}_{q'}=D\rho_{\sigma(q')}\,K^\sigma_{q'}\,D\rho_{\sigma(q')}^\top,\] and no metric on \(Q\) is used. \(K\) is a degenerate cometric (PSD, of rank \(\le\dim Q'\)); when \(\sigma\) is not injective, \(K^\sigma\) is a field along \(\sigma\) and fails to be a cometric on \(Q\).

For LoRA, \(\rho(B,A)=W_0+sBA\) with \(s=\alpha/r\) (\(\alpha/\sqrt r\) for rsLoRA), block rates \(\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\eta_BI,\eta_AI)\) and no LoRA dropout, under gradient flow (equivalently, to first order in the step size for GD), \[K_{(B,A)}(G)=s^2\big(\eta_B\,GA^\top A+\eta_A\,BB^\top G\big).\] Under Adam there is no state-independent linear cometric.

When a change of chart does not matter

A gauge transformation changes the parameters and keeps the weights. It leaves training unchanged exactly when the cometrics agree, which for LoRA’s gauge \(GL_r\) singles out \(O(r)\).

Theorem III.3 (gradient descent is natural exactly where cometrics agree; for LoRA’s linear gauge, exactly along \(O(r)\)). (a) Let \(\varphi:Q\to Q'\) be a diffeomorphism with \(\rho'\circ\varphi=\rho\), and let \(\Lambda,\Lambda'\) be the rate operators on \(Q,Q'\). Under gradient flow, the two methods induce the same \(\theta\)-velocity at \(q\) and at \(\varphi(q)\) for every loss iff \(K^\rho_q=K^{\rho'}_{\varphi(q)}\), that is, \[D\rho'_{\varphi(q)}\big(D\varphi_q\Lambda D\varphi_q^\top-\Lambda'\big)D\rho'^\top_{\varphi(q)}=0.\] This holds whenever \(D\varphi\Lambda D\varphi^\top=\Lambda'\), which is an isometry when \(\Lambda=\Lambda'=I\). That condition is necessary when \(D\rho'_{\varphi(q)}\) is injective, but not in general; every linear gauge of a linear parametrisation is natural, and so is every pointwise-orthogonal non-linear LoRA gauge \(\varphi(q)=\varphi_{g(q)}(q)\), \(g(q)\in O(r)\).

(b) For LoRA’s linear gauge \(\varphi_g(B,A)=(Bg^{-1},gA)\) with block-scalar rates, at points where \(A\) has full row rank (\(B\) arbitrary, including \(B=0\)) and \(r\lt n\), the criterion holds iff \(g\in O(r)\). The same holds symmetrically where \(B\) has full column rank and \(r\lt m\).

(a) Put \(\ell=L\circ\rho'\), so that \(L\circ\rho=\ell\circ\varphi\). The \(\rho\)-flow is \(\dot q=-\Lambda D\varphi^\top\nabla\ell\), so \(\tfrac{d}{dt}\varphi(q)=-D\varphi\Lambda D\varphi^\top\nabla\ell\). For the examples, a linear \(\rho\) has constant \(D\rho\), and \(D\rho\,P=D\rho\) for a linear gauge \(P\); for instance, for \(\rho(x,y)=\theta_0+x+y\) the shear \((x,y)\mapsto(2x,y-x)\) preserves \(\rho\) and \(K\) without being orthogonal. Here \(\operatorname{rank}X\le r\lt n\), so \(c=0\), and full row rank of \(A\) gives \(g^\top g=I\). Only the full row rank of \(A\) is used.

Isometries and charges

Split LoRA’s gauge in two. With per-block scalar rates, the antisymmetric generators rotate the rank channels and preserve the rate metric, so the optimizer cannot feel them. Along the symmetric ones, gradient flow conserves a quadratic charge; for LoRA the charges form one matrix \(\Phi\), whose vanishing is the balancedness of deep linear networks.

Theorem III.5 (Noether charges and isometries for pulled-back signals). Known Let a Lie group act linearly on \(Q=\mathbb R^N\) with \(\rho\circ k=\rho\), let \(\Lambda\succ0\) be constant, and let \(\gamma(t)\in T^*_{\rho(q(t))}\Theta\) be any cotangent signal, gradient or not. Consider the continuous-time updates \[\dot q=-\Lambda\,D\rho_q^\top\gamma(t).\]

(i) For every generator \(\xi\) with \(\Lambda^{-1}\xi\) symmetric, the charge \(J_\xi(q)=\tfrac12\langle q,\Lambda^{-1}\xi q\rangle\) is conserved.

(ii) For every generator \(\xi\) with \(\Lambda^{-1}\xi\) antisymmetric, \(k=e^{\tau\xi}\) preserves \(\langle\cdot,\Lambda^{-1}\cdot\rangle\), so \(K_{kq}=K_q\): for the same cotangent \(\gamma\), the \(\theta\)-velocities at \(q\) and at \(kq\) agree. Suppose moreover that \(\gamma\) depends on \(q\) only through \((t,\rho(q))\), as \(\nabla_\theta L\) or an open-loop signal does, is locally Lipschitz in \(\theta\), and that \(\rho\in C^{1,1}_{\rm loc}\). Then the \(\theta\)-trajectories from \(q_0\) and from \(kq_0\) coincide. A signal that uses Euclidean norms on \(Q\), as global-norm clipping does, keeps this when \(k\) is also a Euclidean isometry and can break it otherwise.

(iii) When \(\mathfrak g\) is closed under \(\xi\mapsto\Lambda\xi^\top\Lambda^{-1}\), every generator splits into parts of types (i) and (ii), a Cartan decomposition \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak p\); this holds for LoRA’s \(\mathfrak{gl}_r\) with per-block scalar rates. Without closure some generators are of neither type. With per-rank-channel rates, closure holds iff the products \(\eta_{B,j}\eta_{A,j}\) are all equal; when they are pairwise distinct, only diagonal \(X\) give charges and no nonzero \(X\) gives an isometry.

Laws valid for every loss and dataset are characterised by Marcotte, Gribonval and Peyré, 2023; the principle goes back to Du, Hu and Lee, 2018, Arora, Cohen and Hazan, 2018 and Kunin et al., 2021. A non-gradient \(\gamma\) adds little, as \(\gamma(t)\) is the gradient of \(\langle\gamma(t),\theta\rangle\). New here are the PEFT instances.

Invariance of \(\rho\) gives \(D\rho_q(\xi q)=0\). Hence \[\tfrac{d}{dt}J_\xi=\langle\Lambda^{-1}\xi q,\dot q\rangle=-\langle\xi q,D\rho_q^\top\gamma\rangle=-\langle D\rho_q\xi q,\gamma\rangle=0.\]

For (ii), \(\Lambda^{-1}\xi\) antisymmetric gives \(\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\big(e^{\tau\xi^\top}\Lambda^{-1}e^{\tau\xi}\big)=0\), equivalently \(k\Lambda k^\top=\Lambda\). From \(\rho\circ k=\rho\) we get \(D\rho_q=D\rho_{kq}k\), so \[K_q=D\rho_{kq}k\Lambda k^\top D\rho_{kq}^\top=K_{kq},\qquad \tfrac{d}{dt}(kq)=-k\Lambda k^\top D\rho_{kq}^\top\gamma=-\Lambda D\rho_{kq}^\top\gamma.\] When \(\gamma\) factors through \((t,\rho)\), \(kq(t)\) solves the same ODE as the trajectory from \(kq_0\); under the stated regularity the solution is unique.

(iii) Write \[\xi=\tfrac12(\xi+\Lambda\xi^\top\Lambda^{-1})+\tfrac12(\xi-\Lambda\xi^\top\Lambda^{-1}).\] For per-channel rates \(\Lambda(B,A)=(BD_B,D_AA)\), \(\Lambda^{-1}\xi_X(B,A)=(BXD_B^{-1},-D_A^{-1}XA)\) is symmetric (antisymmetric) iff \(XD_B^{-1}\) and \(D_A^{-1}X\) both are. Entrywise, \(X_{ji}=\pm X_{ij}\eta_{B,i}/\eta_{B,j}\) and \(X_{ji}=\pm X_{ij}\eta_{A,j}/\eta_{A,i}\), which admit \(X_{ij}\ne0\) iff \(\eta_{B,i}\eta_{A,i}=\eta_{B,j}\eta_{A,j}\). So an off-diagonal pair gives a charge and an isometry exactly when its products agree, and closure holds iff all products agree.

The balanced slice

In Kempf–Ness theory, the least-norm vectors on a closed orbit of a reductive group form one orbit of its compact part, and they are where the moment map vanishes. For LoRA the moment map is \(\mu=B^\top B-AA^\top\), and the least-norm representatives are the balanced ones.

Proposition III.7 (Kempf–Ness slice; Loewner floor). Known (a) On a full-rank \(GL_r\)-orbit, the critical points of \(\|B\|^2+\|A\|^2\) are exactly the balanced representatives, those with \(\mu=B^\top B-AA^\top=0\). They form one \(O(r)\)-orbit, they are the minima, and the minimum value is \(2\|BA\|_*\).

(b) Assume gradient flow with block-scalar rates \(\eta_A,\eta_B\), no weight decay, a loss that depends on \((B,A)\) only through \(BA\), and any cotangent signal of the form \(\Lambda D\rho^\top\gamma\) (dropout, minibatches and clipping included). From \(B_0=0\), \[B^\top B=\tfrac{\eta_B}{\eta_A}\big(AA^\top-A_0A_0^\top\big)\succeq0,\] so \(AA^\top\succeq A_0A_0^\top\) for all time; the \(A\)-factor never shrinks below its initialisation in the Loewner order. \(AA^\top(t)\) need not be monotone in \(t\); only this floor is guaranteed. Discrete steps break the bound by \(O(\eta^2)\) per step. Under Adam it is not guaranteed and fails empirically. With per-time decay \(\lambda\), only \(AA^\top\succeq e^{-2\lambda t}A_0A_0^\top\) holds.

Two regimes and a crossover

When the charge is a multiple of the identity, the LoRA flow has a closed form. Below a scale \(\sigma^*\), one factor carries the motion, as in LoRA-FA; above it, the factors share the work, as in balanced deep linear networks.

The hyperparameter torus

Rescale block \(i\) by \(c_i\) and divide \(s\) by the matching power of the \(c_i\). The method is the same object of \(\mathsf{Meth}\), and training is the same if every step rescales alike. Adam ignores the size of a block’s gradient, so its rate must scale like \(c_i\); an SGD step scales with the gradient, which shrinks by \(c_i\), so its rate must scale like \(c_i^2\).

Theorem III.10 (the hyperparameter torus). Known in part Let \(\rho(q)=\theta_b+s\,\delta(q_1,\dots,q_k)\) with \(\delta\) multihomogeneous of multidegree \((d_1,\dots,d_k)\). Train block \(i\) from \(q_i(0)=\sigma_iu_i\) with Adam (\(\beta_1\in[0,1)\), \(\beta_2\in(0,1)\), bias correction allowed) at learning rate \(\eta_i(t)\), on the loss \(L\circ\rho\) with no \(q\)-dependent regulariser. Assume no gradient clipping (or per-block clipping with thresholds rescaled as below), common random numbers (the same data order and dropout masks in both runs), and exact arithmetic (the result is bit-exact in floating point when every \(c_i\) is a power of \(2\)). Then the trajectory \(t\mapsto\rho(q(t))\) is invariant under the torus action of \(c\in\mathbb R_{\gt0}^k\): \[\big(s,\ \eta_i(t),\ \sigma_i,\ \varepsilon_i,\ \lambda_i,\ C_i\big)\mapsto\Big(s\prod_ic_i^{-d_i},\ c_i\eta_i(t),\ c_i\sigma_i,\ \varepsilon_i/c_i,\ \lambda_i/c_i,\ C_i/c_i\Big).\]

Here \(\varepsilon_i\) is a per-block Adam \(\varepsilon\) (with the convention \(0/0=0\) at \(\varepsilon=0\)), \(\lambda_i\) a per-block decoupled (AdamW) weight decay, and \(C_i\) a per-block clipping threshold. Under SGD, with or without heavy-ball or Nesterov momentum, the same holds with \(\eta_i(t)\mapsto c_i^2\eta_i(t)\) and coupled \(L^2\) decay \(\lambda_i\mapsto\lambda_i/c_i^2\). In particular, any learning-rate schedule shared by all blocks, warm-up included, is preserved.

The hierarchy \(B_r\subset O(r)\subset GL_r\) is strict for \(r\ge2\). A \(GL_r\)-equivariant rule, or any rule mapping gauge orbits to gauge orbits, defines an optimizer on the model itself; \(O(r)\)- and \(B_r\)-equivariant rules in general do not. The default, Adam, sees a finite shadow of the gauge.

What a zero-B start chooses

Default LoRA starts at \(B_0=0\) with a random full-rank \(A_0\). The image remembers only \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\) (Thm II.2). Training remembers what the optimizer’s gauge group cannot move.

Corollary III.13 (what a zero-\(B\) initialisation really chooses). At \(q_0=(0,A_0)\) with \(\operatorname{rank}A_0=r\), with hyperparameters shared by the rank channels and the same data order and dropout masks, two choices of \(A_0\) give the same \(\theta\)-trajectory whenever the optimizer’s gauge group relates them. The resulting invariant of \(A_0\) is:
  • \(\mathrm{row}A_0\in\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)\) for an update rule that is \(GL_r\)-equivariant at every point of its trajectory, including \(B=0\) and rank-deficient \(B\), run without global-norm clipping. An example is \(\delta B=-\eta\nabla_B(AA^\top)^{-1}\), \(\delta A=-\eta(AA^\top)\nabla_A\), which needs only \(\operatorname{rank}A=r\);
  • the PSD matrix \(A_0^\top A_0\) for SGD, which is exactly the initial preconditioner \(\dot W(0)=-s^2\eta_BGA_0^\top A_0\);
  • \(A_0\) up to signed permutation of its rows for Adam.

Conversely, different invariants give different dynamics, for SGD and for Adam at \(\varepsilon=0\).

Random-\(A\) LoRA and EVA are different points of the \(B=0\) stratum \(\{(0,A):\operatorname{rank}A=r\}\) of the apex fibre \(\rho^{-1}(\theta_0)=\{(B,A):BA=0\}\); Init[B] lies in another stratum of the same fibre. LoRA-FA is a different object, the sub-object \(\mathrm{FA}(A_0)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{LoRA}_{(0,A_0)}\) (Prop I.8). More precisely, EVA is a point of the apex fibre of LoRA\(_{r_l}\) at its per-layer redistributed rank and adjusted \(\alpha\), with orthonormal rows only when whiten=False.

Only the pointwise “\(\supseteq\)” half of Thm III.12 is used. For SGD and Adam it holds off the full-rank locus, and the initial Adam state \((m,v)=0\) is fixed by \(B_r\). With \(B_0=0\) the term \(B_0g^{-1}\) stays \(0\), so equivariant updates give identical \(W\)-trajectories. Orthogonal \(g\), hence \(O(r)\) and \(B_r\), preserve gradient norms and so commute with global-norm clipping; a general \(g\in GL_r\) does not. Left \(O(r)\)-orbits of full-rank \(A_0\) are classified by \(A_0^\top A_0\) (Thm II.5(2)), and \(GL_r\)-orbits by the row space (proof of Thm II.2). For the example rule, \(\nabla_{A'}=g^{-\top}\nabla_A\) and \(A'A'^\top=gAA^\top g^\top\) give \(\delta A'=g\,\delta A\), and \(\delta B'=\delta B\,g^{-1}\) as in Prop III.4(a).

The converse compares first steps. Under SGD the first step from \((0,A_0)\) is exactly \(\delta W_1=-s^2\eta_BGA_0^\top A_0\), so equal trajectories for every linear loss force \(A_0^\top A_0=A_0'^\top A_0'\). Under Adam at \(\varepsilon=0\) it is \(\delta W_1=-s\eta_B\,\mathrm{sign}(GA_0^\top)A_0\), and row by row \(F(x)=\mathrm{sign}(xA_0^\top)A_0\) is constant off the hyperplanes \(a_j^\perp\) and jumps by \(\pm2a_j\) across each. So \(F\) determines the hyperplanes, hence each row up to a factor, and the size of its jump fixes that factor up to sign: \(A_0'\) is a signed row permutation of \(A_0\).

Num SGD from \(A_0\) and \(QA_0\) agree to \(10^{-15}\), and from \(A_0\) and \(DA_0\) differ by \(0.81\). Adam from \(A_0\) and \(PA_0\) agree to \(10^{-16}\), and from \(A_0\) and \(QA_0\) differ by \(0.98\). The example rule from \(A_0\) and \(gA_0\) agrees to \(3\times10^{-15}\). LoRA-RITE’s Algorithm 1, run as written with its pseudo-inverses from \(B_0=0\), gives the same weights from \(A_0\) and from \(gA_0\) for a generic \(g\) to \(10^{-13}\), and weights that differ by \(O(1)\) for a different row space.
