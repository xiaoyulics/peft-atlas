Exposé II.A

What a method can say, and where it starts

Expressivity is read off the germ of a method’s image at the pretrained point. An initialisation decides whether that point sits at the apex of a cone, on a smooth sheet of one, or just off the image.

A method is an arrow before it is a thing. Ask what it sees of the point where it lands.

after Grothendieck
In one minute
  • Near the pretrained weights, a method reaches the local shape of its image, a linear space, a cone or a piece of a group orbit. Its first-step directions \(S_1\) and its expressive dimension tell whether training starts at an apex.
  • For \(r\lt\min(m,n)\), full-rank LoRA initialisations come in exactly three kinds up to reparametrisation, classified as sets by \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\) (\(B_0=0\)), \(\mathrm{col}\,B_0\) (\(A_0=0\)) or the subtracted product \(B_0A_0\) (PiSSA-type splits).
  • Zero-factor starts sit at the apex of a cone with \(mr\) or \(nr\) first-order directions, split starts at a smooth point with all \(r(m+n-r)\), which for a \(4096\times4096\) matrix at rank 16 is 130,816 against 65,536. Neither kind reaches everything the other reaches.
  • No exact start of LoRA\(_r\) reaches an update of rank above \(2r\). A factor behind a closed gate, such as \(A\) when \(B_0=0\), does not move the weights at first order.

The image germ

Exposé I turned a PEFT method into a pointed map \(\rho:(Q,q_0)\to(\Theta,\theta_0)\). Its image \(\mathrm{Im}\,M=\rho(Q)\) is everything it can reach. Fine-tuning travels only a short way from the pretrained weights, so what matters is the image near \(\theta_0\), its germ at \(\theta_0\).

The germ takes few shapes. It is a linear space when \(\rho\) is affine, as for BitFit, LoRA-FA, LoRA-XS, FourierFT and (IA)³. It is a cone when \(\rho\) is multihomogeneous and non-linear and starts where the update vanishes, as LoRA’s \(BA\) does at zero initialisation. It is a piece of a group orbit for exactly orthogonal methods such as exact-Cayley OFT. DoRA adds an output gain to LoRA’s cone (Prop II.8), and HRA meets that cone with a rotation orbit (Thm II.7), each under its stated hypotheses. Adapters and prefixes change the network itself, and their germs live among functions (Exposé VI).

Four invariants of the germ carry most of the story, and isomorphic methods share them (Obs I.6).

  • The first-order image \(S_1(M)=d\rho_{q_0}(T_{q_0}Q)\), the directions a first step can take.
  • The type of the base point: regular when the first-order deficit \(d(M)-\dim S_1(M)\) vanishes, an apex when it is positive (Def I.5).
  • The maximum rank of an update, bounded by a minimum cut (Thm II.10).
  • The expressive dimension \(d(M)=\dim\mathrm{Im}\,M\), which falls short of the parameter count \(\lvert M\rvert\) by the gauge waste.

II.A asks where a method starts, II.B what a group-type method can never change (Thm II.5, Cor II.6), and II.C how rank differs from dimension (Prop II.12). Expressivity here means reach, and finding a reachable point is left to Exposé III.

CreditWhich functions a LoRA-adapted network can represent is studied by Zeng & Lee, 2023. The intrinsic dimension of Li et al., 2018 and Aghajanyan et al., 2020 depends on the data, while \(d(M)\) is a property of the method alone.

II.A Initialisations: pointings and strata

Every LoRA recipe decides where training starts. LoRA sets \(B_0=0\) and draws \(A_0\) at random. PiSSA takes both factors from the top of the SVD of \(W_0\) and subtracts their product from the frozen weight, LoRA-GA does the same with the first gradient, and CorDA, OLoRA, MiLoRA and EVA choose otherwise. Each recipe is a pointing, a parameter \(q_0\) with the frozen residual that makes \(\rho(q_0)=\theta_0\), and two pointings of one formula can be different objects of \(\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0)\). Figure 2 draws the typical cases.

Translates of one shape

An additive method adds updates from a fixed shape \(C\), for LoRA the cone \(\mathcal M_{\le r}\) of matrices of rank at most \(r\). Pointing it at \(q\) slides the shape so that \(\delta(q)\) lands on \(\theta_0\), and reachable updates become differences of two points of \(C\).

Theorem II.1 (pointings of an additive method). Let \(M=(Q,q_0,\theta_0+\delta)\) be additive with shape \(C=\delta(Q)\). Write \(-v+C\) for a translate, and \(C-C:=\{c-c':c,c'\in C\}\) for the Minkowski difference.

(a) For each \(q\in Q\), the re-pointed method \(M_q:=(Q,q,\theta_0-\delta(q)+\delta(\cdot))\), with frozen residual \(\theta_0-\delta(q)\), is pointed at \(\theta_0\), and \(\mathrm{Im}\,M_q=\theta_0+(-\delta(q)+C)\).

(b) \(\bigcup_q\mathrm{Im}\,M_q=\theta_0+(C-C)\).

(c) For LoRA\(_r\) on a single linear box with \(s\ne0\), \(C-C=\mathcal M_{\le\min(2r,m,n)}\); for several boxes, \(C-C\) is the product of the per-box varieties \(\mathcal M_{\le\min(2r,m_i,n_i)}\). Hence no exact pointing of LoRA\(_r\) (frozen residual \(\theta_0-\delta(q)\)), whether zero-init or split (PiSSA, OLoRA, CorDA, LoRA-GA), makes an update \(\theta-\theta_0\) of rank \(\gt 2r\) reachable in any adapted matrix. More robustly, from any starting point \(q_0\) of LoRA\(_r\), pointed or not, \(\rho(q)-\rho(q_0)=\delta(q)-\delta(q_0)\) has rank \(\le\min(2r,m,n)\). Quantised-residual schemes (QLoRA, LoftQ, QPiSSA) are pointed only up to a defect that depends on the order of operations (Prop V.2). Measured from \(\theta_0\), their updates contain it, and its rank is generically at least \(\min(m,n)-r\). The bound \(2r\) is attained by split pointings when \(2r\le\min(m,n)\).

The theorem adds that it loses nothing and that rank \(2r\) is needed in general.

(a) At \(q\) the re-pointed map gives \(\theta_0-\delta(q)+\delta(q)=\theta_0\), and its image is \(\theta_0-\delta(q)+C\). (b) Take the union over \(q\) of \(\theta_0+(C-\delta(q))\).

(c) For \(s\ne0\) the shape is \(C=s\,\mathcal M_{\le r}=\mathcal M_{\le r}\). The inclusion \(C-C\subseteq\mathcal M_{\le\min(2r,m,n)}\) is \(\operatorname{rank}(X-Y)\le\operatorname{rank}X+\operatorname{rank}Y\le 2r\). Conversely, \(C\) is a cone and so closed under negation. Split the SVD of a matrix \(Z\) of rank at most \(\min(2r,m,n)\) as \(Z=Z_1+Z_2\) with \(\operatorname{rank}Z_i\le r\), and write \(Z=Z_1-(-Z_2)\). With several boxes \(\delta\) acts box by box, so \(C\) and \(C-C\) are products. The robust form is the same inclusion applied to \(\delta(q)-\delta(q_0)\). For the defects, QLoRA’s \(e\) and QPiSSA’s \(\kappa(W_{\rm res})-W_{\rm res}\) are quantisation errors, generically of full rank, and one-step LoftQ’s \(e-s\,\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\) has rank at least \(\operatorname{rank}e-r\). For attainment, a split pointing with \(P=\delta(q_0)\) of rank \(r\) has image \((\theta_0-P)+\mathcal M_{\le r}\), so it reaches \(\theta_0+(Y-P)\) for every \(Y\) of rank \(r\), and \(Y-P\) has rank \(2r\) for generic \(Y\) when \(2r\le\min(m,n)\).∎

An initialisation chooses which translate of the shape passes through \(\theta_0\). Split initialisations gain updates \(Y-P\) of rank up to \(2r\) and lose generic updates of every rank \(\ge1\) (Thm II.2(d)). Moving a weight matrix by more than rank \(2r\) needs rebasing, as in ReLoRA (Thm V.3), or another shape.

Three strata

Isomorphisms of methods are pointed diffeomorphisms over \(\Theta\) (Def I.2), such as the gauge \((B,A)\mapsto(Bg^{-1},gA)\), \(g\in GL_r\). They preserve the image and \(S_1\) (Obs I.6) but may change training (Exposé III). Up to isomorphism, a full-rank LoRA initialisation is determined by one subspace or one matrix.

Theorem II.2 (three strata of LoRA initialisations). Let \(\delta(B,A)=BA\) with \(1\le r\lt\min(m,n)\). Consider pointed LoRA objects in three distinguished strata of full-rank pointings:
  • \((\mathrm T_A)\): \(B_0=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}A_0=r\);
  • \((\mathrm T_B)\): \(A_0=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}B_0=r\);
  • \((\mathrm S)\): \(\operatorname{rank}B_0A_0=r\), with frozen residual \(\theta_0-B_0A_0\).

(a) Classification. Within a stratum, \(M_{q_0}\cong M_{q_0'}\) iff, respectively, \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0=\mathrm{row}\,A_0'\); \(\mathrm{col}\,B_0=\mathrm{col}\,B_0'\); or \(B_0A_0=B_0'A_0'\). Objects in different strata are never isomorphic. As a set, the isomorphism classes of the three strata therefore form

\[\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathbb R^n)\ \sqcup\ \mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathbb R^m)\ \sqcup\ \mathcal M_r(m\times n).\]

(b) First order. \(\dim S_1\) is \(mr\), \(nr\) and \(r(m+n-r)\) respectively, while \(d=r(m+n-r)\) in every case. The deficits are \(r(n-r)\), \(r(m-r)\) and \(0\).

(c) Geometry. In \(\mathrm T_A\) and \(\mathrm T_B\), \(\mathrm{Im}=\theta_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\), whose vertex \(\theta_0\) is an apex. In \(\mathrm S\), \(\mathrm{Im}=(\theta_0-P)+\mathcal M_{\le r}\) with \(P=B_0A_0\), and \(\theta_0\) is a smooth point.

(d) Incomparability. The images of \(\mathrm S\)-type and \(\mathrm T\)-type objects are incomparable.

(e) Scope. The three strata do not exhaust the pointings of LoRA\(_r\). Outside them lie: \(B_0=0\) with \(\operatorname{rank}A_0\lt r\); \(A_0=0\) with \(\operatorname{rank}B_0\lt r\); \(B_0=A_0=0\); \(0\lt\operatorname{rank}B_0A_0\lt r\); and pointings with \(B_0,A_0\) both nonzero and \(B_0A_0=0\). HF PEFT ships one of the last kind: for even \(r\) and in exact arithmetic, init_lora_weights="orthogonal" has \((\operatorname{rk}B_0,\operatorname{rk}A_0,\operatorname{rk}B_0A_0)=(r/2,r/2,0)\). It is an apex with \(\dim S_1=\tfrac r2(m+n-\tfrac r2)\), isomorphic to no object of the three strata. In bf16 the two factors are rounded separately, so \(B_0A_0\) is small but nonzero and this init becomes a generic pointing with a tiny defect. For every pointing, the image determines \(P=B_0A_0\) (its vertex is \(\theta_0-P\)), and \(S_1=\{XA_0+B_0Y\}\) determines \((\mathrm{row}\,A_0,\mathrm{col}\,B_0)\) whenever these are proper subspaces.

  • “If” in (a). Two full-row-rank matrices with the same row space satisfy \(A_0'=gA_0\) for a unique \(g\in GL_r\), and \((B,A)\mapsto(Bg^{-1},gA)\) is a pointed isomorphism over \(\Theta\). \(\mathrm T_B\) is symmetric. In \(\mathrm S\), the full-rank factorisations of \(P\) form one \(GL_r\)-orbit, and the residuals agree.
  • “Only if” in (a). In \(\mathrm T_A\), \(d\rho_{(0,A_0)}(\dot B,\dot A)=\dot BA_0\), so \(S_1=\{XA_0\}\) is the set of matrices whose row space lies in \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\). It determines \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\), and it is an isomorphism invariant by Obs I.6. \(\mathrm T_B\) is symmetric. In \(\mathrm S\), the image \((\theta_0-P)+\mathcal M_{\le r}\) is an isomorphism invariant, and a translate of \(\mathcal M_{\le r}\) determines its vertex. Suppose \(c+\mathcal M_{\le r}=c'+\mathcal M_{\le r}\) with \(D=c-c'\ne0\), so that \(D+\mathcal M_{\le r}=\mathcal M_{\le r}\), and let \(k=\operatorname{rank}D\). If \(k\gt r\), take \(X=0\). Otherwise take \(X\) of rank \(r-k+1\) whose column and row spaces meet those of \(D\) only in \(0\), which is possible since \(r+1\le\min(m,n)\). Then \(\operatorname{rank}(D+X)=r+1\) in both cases, a contradiction. Hence the image determines \(P\), for every pointing.
  • Separating the strata. \(S_1(\mathrm T_A)=\{Z:\mathrm{row}\,Z\subseteq V\}\) contains \(e_iv^\top\) for every \(i\) and every \(v\in V\), so it can equal \(S_1(\mathrm T_B)=\{Z:\mathrm{col}\,Z\subseteq U\}\) only if \(U=\mathbb R^m\), which is impossible since \(r\lt m\). When \(m\ne n\) the dimensions \(mr\ne nr\) already differ. \(\mathrm S\) differs from both because \(\dim S_1=r(m+n-r)\) differs from \(mr\) (as \(n\gt r\)) and from \(nr\) (as \(m\gt r\)).

  • (c). Classically, \(\mathcal M_r\) is open in \(\mathcal M_{\le r}\) and is a manifold of dimension \(r(m+n-r)\), and \(\mathrm{Sing}\,\mathcal M_{\le r}=\mathcal M_{\le r-1}\). So \(P\), of rank \(r\), is a smooth point, and \(\theta_0\) is a smooth point of the \(\mathrm S\)-image. In the \(\mathrm T\) strata, \(\theta_0\) is the vertex of the non-linear cone \(\theta_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\), a singular point (Def I.5), and by (b) its deficit is positive, so it is an apex.
  • (d). A generic rank-one \(X\) satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}(P+X)=\operatorname{rank}(X-P)=r+1\). So \(\theta_0+X\) lies in the \(\mathrm T\)-image but not in the \(\mathrm S\)-image, and \(\theta_0-P+X\) lies in the \(\mathrm S\)-image but not in the \(\mathrm T\)-image.
  • (e). The dimension is Prop II.3. For any pointing, \(S_1=\{XA_0+B_0Y\}\) is the space of matrices that send \((\mathrm{row}\,A_0)^\perp\) into \(\mathrm{col}\,B_0\). Its Frobenius orthogonal complement is \((\mathrm{col}\,B_0)^\perp\otimes(\mathrm{row}\,A_0)^\perp\), whose column and row spans recover \((\mathrm{col}\,B_0)^\perp\) and \((\mathrm{row}\,A_0)^\perp\) when both subspaces are proper. HF’s orthogonal init draws one orthogonal \(Q\in O(r)\) and sets \(A_0=(G_AQ')^\top/10\) and \(B_0=G_BQ''/10\) with Gaussian \(G_A,G_B\), where \(Q'\) and \(Q''\) are the odd- and even-numbered rows of \(Q\) (HF’s q_odd and q_even). Distinct rows of \(Q\) are orthogonal, so \(B_0A_0=0\), with both factors of rank \(r/2\). Its image is \(\theta_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\), with vertex \(\theta_0\), so it is not in \(\mathrm S\). Its pair \((\mathrm{col}\,B_0,\mathrm{row}\,A_0)\) has dimensions \((r/2,r/2)\), against \((0,r)\) for \(\mathrm T_A\) and \((r,0)\) for \(\mathrm T_B\), so its \(S_1\) differs from theirs. Its deficit is \(\tfrac r2(m+n-\tfrac{3r}2)\gt0\). In low precision the separately rounded factors acquire small extra singular values, so the placement holds only up to a small perturbation.

Numerical check: \(\dim S_1=(14,10,20)\) for \((\mathrm T_A,\mathrm T_B,\mathrm S)\) at \((m,n,r)=(7,5,2)\), and \(11\) for HF’s orthogonal init.∎

Analogy (a moduli space of initialisations). Calling the three spaces a moduli space borrows a word. The bijection is of sets only. \(\mathrm T_A\) lies in the closure of \(\mathrm S\), since \((\varepsilon B',A_0)\to(0,A_0)\), so the quotient is not a topological disjoint union, and no universal family is built.

More: the first-order deficit and the data

\(S_1\) counts directions in weight space. The lazy-regime kernel \(J_f\,d\rho\,\Lambda\,d\rho^\top J_f^\top\) also involves the network Jacobian \(J_f\) and the optimizer’s metric \(\Lambda\), and for random-\(A_0\) zero-init LoRA it approximates the full fine-tuning kernel (Malladi et al., 2023). A large first-order deficit need not cost much on the data.

Counting first-order directions

A first step of LoRA from \((B,A)\) adds \(s(\dot BA+B\dot A)\), a matrix with rows in \(\mathrm{row}\,A\) plus one with columns in \(\mathrm{col}\,B\). Counting both families and their overlap gives \(\dim S_1\).

Proposition II.3 (first-order rank formula: smoothness, alignment and scale). For \(\kappa(B,A)=W_{\rm base}+sBA\) with \(s\ne0\),
\[\operatorname{rank}d\kappa_{(B,A)}=m\operatorname{rk}A+n\operatorname{rk}B-\operatorname{rk}A\operatorname{rk}B\qquad\text{for all }(B,A),\]

and \(\{XA\}\cap\{BY\}=\{BRA:R\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}\}\). For full-rank frames this overlap is the tangent space of the product \(\mathrm{FA}(A)\times\mathrm{FB}(B)\) of Prop I.8(a), re-based at \(\kappa(B,A)\), whose image is the meet of the two one-sided images. It is a generalised LoRA-XS, and LoRA-XS proper when \((B,A)\) span the top-\(r\) singular subspaces of \(W_0\) (with \(\sigma_r\gt0\)), as at a PiSSA init.

Under gradient flow \(\dot B=-\eta_B\nabla_BL\), \(\dot A=-\eta_A\nabla_AL\), with \(G=\nabla_WL\),

\[\tfrac{d}{dt}L=-s^2\big(\eta_B\|GA^\top\|_F^2+\eta_A\|B^\top G\|_F^2\big)\]

at every time, in particular at \((B_0,A_0)\). To first order in the step sizes the same holds for full-batch gradient descent, and in expectation for unbiased minibatch SGD. Under Adam (\(\varepsilon\to0\)) the first step instead lowers the loss, to first order in the step sizes, by \(s\big(\eta_B\|GA_0^\top\|_1+\eta_A\|B_0^\top G\|_1\big)\) for \(s\gt0\) (by the same expression with \(\lvert s\rvert\) in general): entrywise \(\ell_1\) norms, linear in \(s\).

(expand)

The image of \(d\kappa\) is \(\{XA\}+\{BY\}\), since the factor \(s\ne0\) does not change it. Its summands are the matrices with row space in \(\mathrm{row}\,A\), of dimension \(m\operatorname{rk}A\), and the matrices with column space in \(\mathrm{col}\,B\), of dimension \(n\operatorname{rk}B\). A matrix \(D\) lies in both iff \(D\in\mathrm{col}\,B\otimes\mathrm{row}\,A\), of dimension \(\operatorname{rk}A\operatorname{rk}B\), and then \(D=B(B^+DA^+)A\).

The gradient-flow rate is \(\langle G,\dot W\rangle\) with \(\dot W=-s^2(\eta_BGA^\top A+\eta_ABB^\top G)\), because \(\nabla_BL=sGA^\top\) and \(\nabla_AL=sB^\top G\). Adam’s first step is \(\delta B=-\eta_B\,\mathrm{sign}(sGA^\top)\), so \(\langle G,s\,\delta B\,A\rangle=-\lvert s\rvert\eta_B\|GA^\top\|_1\), and likewise for \(A\).

Numerical check: the rank formula holds in 400 random cases, including rank-deficient factors, and the rate matches finite differences.∎

Example (one attention projection of a 7B model). At \(4096\times4096\) with \(r=16\), zero-factor initialisations (LoRA, QLoRA, EVA, LoRA-FA) have \(mr=65{,}536\) first-order directions and split ones \(r(m+n-r)=130{,}816\), the dimension of LoRA’s image.

“Better initialisation” mixes three mechanisms, and the proposition separates them.

  • Smoothness. PiSSA and LoRA-GA start off the apex, so their first step sees all \(r(m+n-r)\) directions.
  • Alignment. At a zero-\(B\) start the rate is \(s^2\eta_B\|GA_0^\top\|_F^2\). Among \(A_0\) with orthonormal rows, the top-\(r\) right singular space of \(G\) maximises it, and that is LoRA-GA’s \(A_0\). Its \(B_0\), the next \(r\) left singular vectors, makes the first step approximate full fine-tuning and does not maximise the rate. EVA’s activation frame is a gradient-free proxy. Under Adam the rate depends on the basis of \(A_0\), so this alignment belongs to gradient flow and SGD.
  • Scale. \(\|GA_0^\top\|_F^2\) grows with \(\|A_0\|^2\), and initial scale trades off against learning rate (Thm III.10). HF’s Kaiming default has \(A_0A_0^\top\approx I/3\) and EVA with whiten=False has \(A_0A_0^\top=I\), so fair comparisons normalise \(A_0A_0^\top\).
More: EVA’s frame and the factor scales

EVA’s guarantee assumes inputs independent of the backpropagated errors and bounds the expected per-sample squared gradient norm, a different quantity from the rate above, and HF’s EVA centres the activations. PiSSA’s \(S_r^{1/2}\)-weighted factors and LoRA-GA’s stability scaling also change factor norms (both factors in LoRA-GA’s paper, only \(B\) in HF’s default).

The zero gate

At a zero-\(B\) start, \(BA\) is linear in \(B\) and \(B_0=0\), so moving \(A\) alone changes nothing. The same holds whenever a trainable gate starts closed. Below, \(K=D\rho\,\Lambda\,D\rho^\top\) is the cometric on weights induced by an optimizer’s metric \(\Lambda\) (Exposé III).

Lemma II.4 (zero gate). Let \(Q=U\times V\) with \(U\) a vector space, let \(\rho=\theta_0+\beta(u,v)\) with \(\beta\) smooth, and suppose \(\beta(u_0,v)=0\) for all \(v\); for example \(\beta(\cdot,v)\) linear and \(u_0=0\). Then \(D_v\rho_{q_0}=0\). Hence \(\nabla_v(L\circ\rho)(q_0)=0\), \(S_1=D_u\beta(u_0,v_0)(U)\) (\(=\beta(U,v_0)\) in the linear case), and the cometric at \(q_0\) is \(K_{q_0}=D_u\beta\,\Lambda_{uu}\,D_u\beta^\top\), where \(\Lambda_{uu}\) is the \(uu\)-block of \(\Lambda\); no block-diagonality is needed, since the \(v\)-columns of \(D\rho\) vanish. The weight trajectory \(\theta(t)\) agrees with that of the method with \(v\) frozen at \(v_0\) to first order in \(t\) (gradient flow), or in the step size (one or more Adam steps). The parameter \(v\) itself may still move: under terms not of the form \(L\circ\rho\) (regularisers, decoupled weight decay), and under Adam, whose step-2 move of \(v\) is \(O(\eta)\) although its effect on \(\theta\) is \(O(\eta^2)\).

Function-level variant. For an extension with realised function \(F(u,v,x)\), if \(F(u_0,v,x)=F_0(x)\) for all \(v\) and \(x\), then \(D_vF=0\) at \(q_0\), with the same conclusions at the level of functions. No linearity is needed.

(expand)

Since \(\beta(u_0,v)=0\) for all \(v\), differentiating in \(v\) gives \(D_v\beta(u_0,v_0)=0\). The rest is the chain rule. The gradient \(\nabla_v(L\circ\rho)=D_v\rho^\top\nabla L\) vanishes, \(S_1\) is the image of the \(u\)-columns alone, and in \(K=D\rho\,\Lambda\,D\rho^\top\) every term that touches a \(v\)-column is zero, which leaves \(D_u\beta\,\Lambda_{uu}\,D_u\beta^\top\). The function-level variant is the same argument applied to \(F\).

Numerical check: under Adam with \(\eta\in\{10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4}\}\), the step-2 move of \(A\) in zero-init LoRA scales as \(\eta\), while the deviation of \(\Delta W\) from the frozen-\(A\) method scales as \(\eta^2\).∎

Instances, assuming no weight decay on \(v\):

  • LoRA (\(u=B\)) and VeRA (\(u=b\)).
  • AdaLoRA, with \(u\) its diagonal of singular values started at \(0\). \(\theta\) first moves only along the \(r\) directions \(p_iq_i^\top\), so \(\dim S_1=r\) for generic \(P,Q\). \(P\) and \(Q\) move at step one through the orthogonality penalty (HF default orth_reg_weight=0.5), with no first-order effect on \(\theta\).
  • LoHa with a zero factor (HF zero-initialises hada_w2_b).
  • By the function-level variant, the gate of LLaMA-Adapter and bottleneck adapters whose up-projection starts at zero (Houlsby et al., 2019 start near that point).

HRA’s paired initialisation, though of a different form, shows the same first-order collapse (Thm II.7(b)).

Off the point: quantised bases

QLoRA quantises the frozen weight, so it is LoRA pointed at \(\kappa\theta_0\), with pointing defect \(e=\kappa\theta_0-\theta_0\) (Def V.1, Prop V.2(a)). As \(e\) is generically of full rank, \(\theta_0\) lies outside \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{LoRA}_r(\kappa\theta_0)\) unless \(\operatorname{rank}e\le r\), which is why Theorem II.1 speaks of exact pointings. When the initialisation also splits, the order of operations decides the defect (Prop V.2(b)). Below, \(W\) is the full-precision weight, \(e=\kappa W-W\) and \(\delta_0=\delta(q_0)\).
