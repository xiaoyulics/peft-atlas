How to cite, and how it was made

The entry to cite, the thirty-two papers the atlas leans on most, and how the catalogue and the theorems were built and checked.

Every harvest grows from seeds that other hands have sown. Naming the sowers is part of the work.

after Grothendieck, Récoltes et semailles
In one minute
  • Cite the atlas with the entry in the citation panel, and a result by its number. For a result marked Known, cite the authors named beside it.
  • The 382 methods and 138 papers were checked against arXiv, their venues, the papers themselves and the Hugging Face PEFT source, and 905 corrections were logged.
  • Two referees attacked each of the 41 numbered results; 14 stood and 27 were repaired. A second round re-examined 26 of the repaired ones, and its reports are in theory/.
  • python3 theory/framework_checks.py re-runs the numerical checks in under a minute.

How to cite

Cite the atlas with the entry in the panel below, and give the number of any result you use. “Thm III.10” names the same statement here, in theory/framework.md and in theory/propositions.json. A result marked Known is due to the authors in its margin. We add only its re-derivation and its reading for fine-tuning, so please cite them for the result itself.

Key references

These thirty-two papers carry most of the weight, and each note says where one enters. The citation panel has the full bibliography, and the catalogue a card for every method.

How the catalogue was built

Every entry was written once and then attacked once, by a separate reader looking for what the first got wrong. The literature was split into the thirteen families below, plus four gap-filling batches and two addenda. The 2026 sweep was run in two passes on 2 October 2026, after the authors reported methods missing.

CreditThe family boundaries owe much to earlier maps of the field: the unified view of He et al., 2022, the delta-tuning study of Ding et al., 2023, the guide of Lialin et al., 2023, the survey of Han et al., 2024 and the modular view of Pfeiffer et al., 2023.

Titles, authors and first-version years were checked against the arXiv metadata, and venues against the official records. Formulas and parameter counts were checked against the papers, and against the official code where an initialisation was in doubt. Claims about Hugging Face PEFT were checked against its source at commit 85be3f3 of 30 September 2026. Every change was logged with its reason, 905 in all.

The families overlap, so methods were merged on a canonical identifier and a table of aliases, since matching by name would fold LoRA+ into LoRA and O-LoRA into OLoRA. The BibTeX in the citation panel comes from the arXiv metadata of 493 identifiers, with full author lists, and from hand-written records for the nine entries without one.

Each method then received the seven coordinates of Exposé VIII, read off its formula. The atlas records 403 arrows between methods, each with an explicit map \(h\) satisfying \(\rho_N\circ h=\rho_M\), and 485 obstructions, each naming the test that rules an arrow out.

How the theorems were checked

Each statement was checked twice, for its proof and for its fit to the methods as published and as coded. The framework was synthesised from four proposals and three judge reports. Two independent model referees then attacked each of its 41 numbered statements. Fourteen stood as first written, and 27 were repaired; only the repaired forms appear here.

A second round re-examined 26 of the repaired statements in 48 reports. It raised a major objection to 18, mostly to a side clause: a missing hypothesis, a remark that went past its proof, or a description of a method that matched only one implementation. For example, HF PEFT's LoftQ initialiser turned out to ignore the LoRA scale \(\alpha/r\) (Prop V.2). The statements shown here were revised to what that round supports.

Statuses and first-round notes are in theory/propositions.json, and the second-round reports in theory/round2_verdicts.json. The Rosetta stone shows a result's status when you hover its number.

Reproducing the numbers

python3 theory/framework_checks.py re-runs the [Num] computations (sections 1 to 11 of the script) and the main numbers of the referee round (section 12). It uses float64 PyTorch autograd, measures the dimension of an image as a Jacobian rank, and runs optimizers step by step. It is seeded and takes under a minute. The few remaining numbers come from the referees' scripts and those in theory/proposals/.

Each line prints a value beside its prediction, and some are controls that should fail. Under Adam with \(\varepsilon=0\), LoRA+ with ratio \(\lambda=8\) and plain LoRA with \(\alpha\) multiplied by \(\lambda\) differ by exactly \(0.0\) over 200 steps, bit for bit since \(\lambda\) is a power of two; without the rescaling they differ by \(0.93\). Adam's first step has a defect near \(10^{-15}\) under signed permutations of the rank coordinates, and \(6.6\) and \(11.3\) under a rotation and a diagonal rescaling, as Thm III.12 predicts.

Colophon

The site is one static page that runs from a web host, from disk or in a sandboxed artifact. Its data file is generated from research/ and theory/. MathJax 3.2.2 sets the mathematics, and D3 7.9.0 with a small linear-algebra kernel in plain JavaScript draws the figures. Type is Source Serif 4 for text, headings and the title, IBM Plex Sans for labels and controls, and IBM Plex Mono for numbers and code. Moss marks what holds or merges, seal an obstruction, ochre a condition.

The chapters are called exposés after the numbered talks of Grothendieck's seminars, whose habit we also borrow, of studying the morphism and letting the right generality do the work.

Thanks go to the authors of the 382 methods and 138 papers catalogued here and to the maintainers of Hugging Face PEFT.
