Exposé 0–I

A method is an arrow

We model a weight-space PEFT method as a pointed map into the pretrained weights. The frozen model and full fine-tuning sit at the two ends, and sums and products of such maps already give methods in use.

Look at the arrow, and at the point where it lands. The objects will then explain themselves.

after Grothendieck
In one minute
  • A weight-space PEFT method is a pointed smooth map \(\rho:(Q,q_0)\to(\Theta,\theta_0)\) sending the initialisation to the pretrained weights. \(N\) simulates \(M\) when a pointed smooth \(h\) satisfies \(\rho_N\circ h=\rho_M\).
  • The frozen model is initial and full fine-tuning terminal, with the merge as the arrow into it. Every weight-space method that starts at \(\theta_0\) sits between them.
  • Parameter count is a poor measure. LoRA with \(r=16\) on a \(4096\times4096\) matrix has 131,072 parameters and a 130,816-dimensional image, yet from zero initialisation its first steps use only 65,536 directions.
  • Adding updates gives a symmetric monoidal structure in which RoSA, GraLoRA and concatenated LoRAs are sums. The product of the two one-sided LoRAs with frozen frames is LoRA-XS.

The thesis

Fine-tuning papers usually compare methods by their number of trainable parameters. That number does not say what a method can reach, how it trains, or what merging does. Those are properties of a map.

A pretrained checkpoint \(\theta_0\) is a point in weight space \(\Theta\), and fine-tuning moves it. A parameter-efficient method chooses where the point may move, through a pointed map

\[\rho:(Q,q_0)\longrightarrow(\Theta,\theta_0)\]

from a small space of trainable parameters into weight space, which sends the initialisation \(q_0\) to the checkpoint. LoRA is the map \((B,A)\mapsto W_0+sBA\) on each adapted matrix. Orthogonal fine-tuning (OFT) is \(Q\mapsto W_0\,C(Q)\), where \(C\) is the Cayley map of a skew matrix. Full fine-tuning is the identity of \(\Theta\), and the frozen model is the inclusion of the single point \(\theta_0\). Figure 1 shows where each catalogued method sits relative to these two.

Following Grothendieck, we study a morphism over a base, here the pretrained point. Three invariants of \(\rho\) do most of the work, with one further layer for architectures.

Where the category theory works

Categorical language is cheap, so we name the seven places where it does real work.

  1. The pointed slice gives the interval [frozen, full FT] and makes “simulation” precise (Def I.2, Obs I.4).
  2. Isomorphism invariants finer than the image, \(S_1\) and the singular locus, classify LoRA's full-rank initialisations as a set (Obs I.6, Thm II.2).
  3. A categorical product produces LoRA-XS. Intersections of images, which are not products, organise others such as HRA (Prop I.8, Thm II.7).
  4. Base change along maps that change the function, such as quantisers, creates pointing defects (Prop V.2).
  5. The lens 2-functor \(\mathbf{Para}(\mathsf R)\) separates tangent-side from cotangent-side methods. Its degree-one collapse shows that GaLore under Adam, without weight decay or global gradient clipping, is period by period one-sided LoRA on a moving base with its optimizer state carried over (Prop IV.2, after Torroba-Hennigen et al., 2025).
  6. Naturality. Gradient flow is natural exactly where cometrics agree, which for LoRA's linear gauge with block-scalar rates means along \(O(r)\) where \(A\) has full row rank and \(r\lt n\). A rule combining LoRA factors is well defined exactly when it is constant on the fibres of \((B,A)\mapsto BA\) (Thm III.3, Prop V.4).
  7. Symmetric monoidal structure bounds rank by a min-cut of a tensor-network presentation (Thm II.10).

The rest is real algebraic geometry, invariant theory, Lie generation and gradient-flow ODEs, and we say so where it happens.

More: constructions we leave out

Some constructions are true here and do no work, so we leave them out. They include coends for the rank filtration, a saturation monad, sheaf language for locality, the Grothendieck construction \(\int\mathsf{Meth}\to\mathbf{PNet}\), and coKleisli 2-cells for mixtures.

Pretrained models and methods

A pretrained network is a function with a parameter slot and a chosen value in it. Its natural home is the bicategory of parametrised maps.

Definition I.1 (Para; pretrained model). For a cartesian category \(\mathcal C\), the bicategory \(\mathbf{Para}(\mathcal C)\) has as 1-cells \(X\to Y\) the pairs \((P,\,f:P\times X\to Y)\), and as 2-cells \((P,f)\Rightarrow(Q,g)\) the maps \(r:Q\to P\) with \(g=f\circ(r\times X)\). A pretrained model is a pointed 1-cell \(F=(\Theta,\theta_0,f)\) of \(\mathbf{Para}(\mathbf{Diff})\). Its realisation is \(\Phi_f:\Theta\to C^\infty(X,Y)\), \(\theta\mapsto f(\theta,-)\). Fine-tuning is a path \(\theta(t)\) with \(\theta(0)=\theta_0\).
Credit\(\mathbf{Para}\) and the lens reading of backpropagation are due to Fong, Spivak and Tuyéras, 2017 and Cruttwell, Gavranović, Ghani, Wilson and Zanasi, 2021. See also Gavranović et al., 2024 for a broader programme.
Definition I.2 (methods and simulations). A weight-space PEFT method at \(\theta_0\) is a pointed smooth map \(M=(Q,q_0,\rho)\), \(\rho:Q\to\Theta\), with \(\rho(q_0)=\theta_0\). Usually \(\dim Q\ll\dim\Theta\), and \(\theta_0\) (or a splitting of it) is frozen inside \(\rho\). These form the pointed slice \[\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0):=\mathbf{Diff}_*/(\Theta,\theta_0).\]
  • A morphism \(h:M\to N\) is a pointed smooth map with \(\rho_N\circ h=\rho_M\). Read it as “\(N\) simulates \(M\)”. Then \(N\) reproduces every weight that \(M\) reaches, together with its parameter path. A weak morphism drops the condition \(h(q_0)=q_0'\).
  • The budget is \(\lvert M\rvert=\dim Q\).
  • \(M\) is additive if \(\rho=\theta_0+\delta\) with \(\delta(q_0)=0\) and \(\delta\) independent of \(\theta_0\).
  • \(M\) is of action type if \(\rho(q)=\gamma(q)\cdot\theta_0\) for a map \(\gamma\) into a group acting on \(\Theta\).
More: four objects and one arrow

On one \(m\times n\) box, LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) is \(\rho(B,A)=W_0+sBA\) on \(Q=\mathbb R^{m\times r}\times\mathbb R^{r\times n}\), pointed at \((0,A_0)\). It is additive, with budget \(r(m+n)\). OFT (Qiu et al., 2023) with the exact Cayley map is \(\rho(Q)=W_0\,C(Q)\) for skew \(Q\) (block-diagonal in OFT), pointed at \(Q=0\). It is of action type, with \(\gamma=C\) landing in \(SO(n)\) on the input side. The frozen model is \((\mathrm{pt},*,\theta_0)\), and full fine-tuning is \((\Theta,\theta_0,\mathrm{id})\).

For an arrow, take LoRA-FA (Zhang et al., 2023), \(B\mapsto W_0+sBA_0\) with \(A_0\) frozen. The pointed map \(h(B)=(B,A_0)\) satisfies \(\rho_{\rm LoRA}\circ h=\rho_{\rm FA}\), so LoRA pointed at \((0,A_0)\) simulates LoRA-FA.

CreditRandom-subspace training, \(\rho(z)=\theta_0+Pz\) with fixed random \(P\), is how Li et al., 2018 measured intrinsic dimension and how Aghajanyan et al., 2020 fine-tuned language models in small subspaces. Here it is a linear object with \(d(M)=\lvert M\rvert\) when \(P\) has full column rank.

What Para adds

Remark I.3 (orientation, and what Para adds). Each \(\rho\) is a 2-cell \(F\Rightarrow F^\rho:=(Q,f\circ(\rho\times X))\) out of \(F\). A simulation \(h:M\to N\) is a 2-cell \(F^{\rho_N}\Rightarrow F^{\rho_M}\), since \(\rho_N\circ h=\rho_M\). Hence \(\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0)\) is the opposite of the pointed coslice of reparametrisation 2-cells under \(F\). The slice over \(F\) is a different category, and its objects are extensions (Exposé VI).

\(\mathsf{Meth}\) depends only on \((\Theta,\theta_0)\) and not on \(f\). So for weight-space methods, \(\mathbf{Para}\) is bookkeeping. The network enters in exactly three places, and we use \(\mathbf{Para}\) only there:

  • extensions and mergeability (Def VI.1, Thm VI.3);
  • the realisation \(\Phi_f\), through functional images and descent (Obs II.15);
  • the backpropagation lens (Def IV.1, Prop IV.2).
CreditCalling \(\mathbf{Para}\) bookkeeping concerns this slice only. In the three places listed we use the framework of Fong, Spivak and Tuyéras and Cruttwell et al. directly, including their lens 2-functor in Exposé IV.
More: why the next exposés work in weight space

Reach in \(\Theta\) is a property of the image, and the cometric \(K=D\rho\,\Lambda\,D\rho^\top\) that shapes training is built from \(\rho\) and the optimizer's rates. The network enters training through the gradient it hands to the method, and enters expressivity through \(\Phi_f\) once we ask about functions instead of weights. So Exposés II and III work in \(\Theta\) and flag the places where \(f\) matters.

Frozen and full fine-tuning

Observation I.4 (universal properties). In \(\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0)\):

(a) the frozen model \((\mathrm{pt},*,\theta_0)\) is initial, and full fine-tuning \((\Theta,\theta_0,\mathrm{id})\) is terminal. The unique morphism \(M\to\) Full FT is \(\rho_M\) itself.

(b) If the fibre product \(Q_M\times_\Theta Q_N\) exists in \(\mathbf{Diff}\), it is the product \(M\times N\). It exists, for instance, when the set-level fibre product is an embedded submanifold of \(Q_M\times Q_N\), as for a clean intersection or for affine \(\rho\)'s as in Prop I.8(a). Transversality \(\rho_M\pitchfork\rho_N\) cannot be the reason when \(\lvert M\rvert+\lvert N\rvert\lt\dim\Theta\), since at \((q_0,q_0')\) it would force \(\lvert M\rvert+\lvert N\rvert\ge\dim\Theta\). When the product exists in \(\mathsf{Meth}\), its underlying set is the set-level fibre product, so \(\mathrm{Im}(M\times N)=\mathrm{Im}\,M\cap\mathrm{Im}\,N\). The product can fail to exist. On \(\Theta=\mathbb R\) (\(1\times1\) matrices), \(\mathrm{LoRA}_1\times\text{Frozen}\) would have the cross \(\{BA=0\}\subset\mathbb R^2\) as underlying set. No manifold structure on the cross has the universal property, because both axes would have to lift smoothly through the point over the origin, which forces rank \(2\) there.

(c) \(\mathrm{Im}\) is a functor to the poset of subsets of \(\Theta\) containing \(\theta_0\). Conversely, \(\mathrm{Im}\,M\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,N\) yields a morphism of the set-level slice \(\mathbf{Set}_*/(\Theta,\theta_0)\), though not necessarily a smooth one. Take \(\rho_M(t)=t\) and \(\rho_N(u)=u^3\) on \(\mathbb R\), pointed at \(0\). The images agree, yet no smooth \(h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfies \(\rho_N\circ h=\mathrm{id}\), that is, \(h(t)^3=t\).

(a) The only pointed map \(\mathrm{pt}\to Q\) is \(*\mapsto q_0\), and it lies over \(\Theta\) because \(\rho(q_0)=\theta_0\). A morphism into the terminal object must satisfy \(\mathrm{id}\circ h=\rho_M\).

(b) Products in a pointed slice are pullbacks in \(\mathbf{Diff}_*\). The forgetful functor \(\mathbf{Diff}_*\to\mathbf{Diff}\) creates limits, since \(\mathbf{Diff}_*\) is the coslice \(\mathrm{pt}/\mathbf{Diff}\), and the induced map out of a pointed test object is pointed because its components are. Smooth maps landing in an embedded submanifold are smooth into it, which gives the sufficient condition. Transversality at \((q_0,q_0')\) means \(d\rho_M(T_{q_0})+d\rho_N(T_{q_0'})=T_{\theta_0}\Theta\), and the left side has dimension at most \(\lvert M\rvert+\lvert N\rvert\). A point \(\theta\in\mathrm{Im}\,M\cap\mathrm{Im}\,N\) has preimages \(q,q'\), and \((q,q')\) lies in the fibre product. Testing a product in \(\mathsf{Meth}\) against pointed two-point \(0\)-manifolds shows that its points are exactly the pairs \((q,q')\) over a common \(\theta\).

For \(\mathrm{LoRA}_1\times\text{Frozen}\), suppose a product \(P\) exists, with canonical map \(\iota:P\to\mathbb R^2\), and let \(p^*\) be its unique point over the origin \((0,0)\). The forgetful functor \(\mathbf{Diff}_*\to\mathbf{Diff}\) is a right adjoint (its left adjoint adds a disjoint base point), so \(P\) is also the pullback in \(\mathbf{Diff}\), and unpointed smooth curves into the cross lift to \(P\). The axis curves \(t\mapsto(t,0)\) and \(t\mapsto(0,t)\) lift to curves through \(p^*\), so \(d\iota_{p^*}\) hits \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) and has rank \(2\). Then \(\iota\) is a submersion near \(p^*\), and its image contains an open subset of \(\mathbb R^2\), which the cross does not. The argument covers every pointing of \(\mathrm{LoRA}_1\), including the standard one \((0,a_0)\) with \(a_0\ne0\), whose base point is a smooth point of the cross.

(c) Functoriality is \(\rho_N\circ h=\rho_M\). For the converse, choose (by the axiom of choice) for each \(\theta\in\mathrm{Im}\,M\) a preimage \(s(\theta)\) under \(\rho_N\) with \(s(\theta_0)=q_0'\), and put \(h=s\circ\rho_M\). In the example the only candidate is the cube root, which is not differentiable at \(0\).∎

Every weight-space method that starts at \(\theta_0\) lies in [frozen, full FT]. Adapters, prefixes and ReFT are extensions outside \(\mathsf{Meth}\) (Exposé VI). Merging into full-precision weights is the canonical morphism to the terminal object, so in exact arithmetic it is definitional; quantised bases and combinations of adapters raise real questions (Prop V.2, Prop V.4). The product, when it exists, is the universal method simulated by both factors, with image \(\mathrm{Im}\,M\cap\mathrm{Im}\,N\). Prop I.8(a) computes one, LoRA-XS, while Prop I.8(b) and Thm II.7 compute intersections of images only.

More: defaults that do not start at the checkpoint

Some defaults move the weights before training starts. Hugging Face PEFT's default FourierFT draws its spectrum from a standard normal, and VB-LoRA's default vector bank and logits are random too. LoRA with init_lora_weights=False, LoRA-XS with its small random \(R\), and every method on a quantised base also start away from \(\theta_0\). Each lives over its own starting weight or carries a pointing defect, which Exposé V measures.

More: lifting obstructions beyond first order

The gap between \(\mathbf{Set}\) and \(\mathbf{Diff}\) in (c) is a smooth lifting obstruction. Obs I.6 below detects its first-order instances. In the cube-root example, \(S_1(M)=\mathbb R\not\subseteq S_1(N)=0\). Higher-order obstructions need finer invariants. The maps \(\rho_M(t)=t^3\) and \(\rho_N(u)=u^5\) have equal images and \(S_1=0\) on both sides, yet the only lift, \(t^{3/5}\), is not \(C^1\).

Invariants of a method

The budget \(\lvert M\rvert\) counts parameters. Two other numbers say more, the dimension of what a method reaches and of what it reaches to first order at the start, which is what the first gradient steps use. For LoRA with \(r=16\) on a \(4096\times4096\) matrix they are \(130{,}816\) and, from zero initialisation, \(65{,}536\), against a budget of \(131{,}072\) (Thm II.2, Prop II.12).

Definition I.5 (invariants of an object). For \(M=(Q,q_0,\rho)\):
  • the image \(\mathrm{Im}\,M=\rho(Q)\);
  • the expressive dimension \(d(M)=\dim\mathrm{Im}\,M=\max_q\operatorname{rank}d\rho_q\);
  • the gauge waste \(g(M)=\lvert M\rvert-d(M)\);
  • the first-order image \(S_1(M)=d\rho_{q_0}(T_{q_0}Q)\), and the first-order deficit \(d(M)-\dim S_1(M)\);
  • the gauge group \(\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Diff}(Q):\rho\circ\varphi=\rho\}\), which is unpointed, since a gauge transformation moves \(q_0\) inside its fibre. In practice we exhibit a Lie group acting on \(Q\) that preserves \(\rho\) and is locally transitive on generic fibres, and call it the generic gauge.

The base point \(\theta_0\) is regular for \(M\) if the deficit is \(0\), and an apex if the deficit is positive. The vertex is the subset-level notion, the cone point of an image germ. A cone germ that is a \(C^1\) manifold at its vertex equals its tangent space there, so it is linear. Hence the vertex of a non-linear cone is a singular point.

More: cones in this site, and the word “gauge”

Apex and vertex differ in general. At a crossing of two smooth branches the image is singular, yet the deficit can be \(0\). In every case below with a positive deficit, the image germ is (diffeomorphic to) a cone with vertex \(\theta_0\). The exception is DoRA, which inherits LoRA's deficit through its torus factor.

“Gauge” here means the symmetry group of a parametrisation, as in the definition above. There is no connection, and away from the full-rank stratum the fibres jump. The word borrows the physicist's picture of redundant coordinates that leave every observation unchanged. Only the group and its action enter the theorems.

If \(N\) simulates \(M\), whatever \(M\) can do at its first step, \(N\) can do too.

Observation I.6 (\(S_1\) is functorial). If \(M\to N\) is a (pointed) morphism, then \(S_1(M)\subseteq S_1(N)\). In particular, isomorphic methods have equal \(S_1\) and equal first-order deficit. Monotonicity holds only along pointed morphisms. Along a weak morphism, such as a gauge map moving \(q_0\) inside its fibre, \(S_1\) can change.

Apply the chain rule \(d\rho_N\circ dh=d\rho_M\) at \(q_0\), using \(h(q_0)=q_0'\). If \(h\) is an isomorphism, \(dh_q\) is invertible for every \(q\), so \(\operatorname{rank}d\rho_M(q)=\operatorname{rank}d\rho_N(h(q))\). Hence \(d(M)=d(N)\) (equivalently \(\mathrm{Im}\,M=\mathrm{Im}\,N\)), and since \(dh_{q_0}\) is surjective, \(S_1(M)=S_1(N)\). So the deficits agree.∎

\(S_1\) is what linearised (lazy, NTK) dynamics can use at initialisation, since the lazy kernel \(J_f\,d\rho\,\Lambda\,d\rho^\top J_f^\top\) has range \(J_f(S_1)\). The kernel also depends on \(\Lambda\) and the network Jacobian \(J_f\), and for random-\(A_0\) zero-init LoRA it is close to full fine-tuning's, so a large first-order deficit need not cost much on data. \(S_1\) separates initialisations the image cannot, namely zero-init \(\mathrm T_A\) against \(\mathrm T_B\), and the choice of \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\) (Thm II.2).

Sums

Weight space is a vector space, so running two methods side by side and adding their updates gives a method again.

Observation I.7 (additive monoidal structure). \(\Theta\) is a vector space, so every object of \(\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0)\) can be written \(\rho=\theta_0+\delta\) with \(\delta(q_0)=0\). Put \[M\oplus N:=\big(Q_M\times Q_N,\ (q_0,q_0'),\ \theta_0+\delta_M+\delta_N\big).\] This is a symmetric monoidal structure with the frozen model as unit, and its image is the Minkowski sum \(\theta_0+(\mathrm{Im}\,\delta_M+\mathrm{Im}\,\delta_N)\). It differs from the categorical product, whose image, when the product exists, is \(\cap\) (Obs I.4(b)).

Write \(\mathrm{LoRA}_k\) for LoRA with frozen residual \(\theta_0\); for split pointings the sum below has residual \(\theta_0-P-P'\). Concatenation \(h(B_1,A_1,B_2,A_2)=([B_1\ B_2],[A_1;A_2])\) is an isomorphism \(\mathrm{LoRA}_r\oplus\mathrm{LoRA}_s\cong\mathrm{LoRA}_{r+s}\) of unpointed objects, and a pointed isomorphism onto \(\mathrm{LoRA}_{r+s}\) pointed at \(([B_0\ B_0'],[A_0;A_0'])\). For \(\mathrm T_A\) pointings (\(B_0=0\), \(\operatorname{rank}A_0=r\)), this target pointing has rank \(r+s\) iff \(\mathrm{row}\,A_0\cap\mathrm{row}\,A_0'=0\). It lies in the stratum \(\mathrm T_A\) of Thm II.2 when moreover \(r+s\lt\min(m,n)\).

Standard pointings do not add up. If \(F(r)\) and \(F(2r)\) are standard \(\mathrm T_A\) (or \(\mathrm T_B\)) pointings, which needs \(2r\lt\min(m,n)\), then \(F(r)\oplus F(r)\not\cong F(2r)\) as pointed objects. So when \(F(1)\) and \(F(2)\) are standard, \(r\mapsto F(r)\) is not strong monoidal into pointed objects. It is strong monoidal on unpointed objects, and on pointed objects for the origin pointing \((B_0,A_0)=(0,0)\), where \(S_1=0\), and for the stacked pointings \(F(r)=F(1)^{\oplus r}\).

RoSA (LoRA \(\oplus\) a fixed-mask sparse method), GraLoRA (\(\oplus\) of \(k^2\) block-embedded LoRAs) and ReLoRA, read as \(\mathrm{LoRA}^{\oplus K}\) unrolled in time apart from its full-rank warm start, are \(\oplus\)-words with Minkowski-sum images. A sum of \(k\) rank-\(r\) LoRAs with residual \(\theta_0\) is isomorphic in \(\mathsf{Meth}\) to one LoRA of rank \(kr\). Under \(\alpha/r\) scaling the isomorphism rescales the \(A\) blocks, so at equal \(\alpha\) and learning rate the two train differently. Hugging Face PEFT's cat merge is this rescaled concatenation applied to trained adapters, exact for unit weights and plain \(\alpha/r\) sources.

More: what is and is not a sum, and the cat merge

LoRA-soup CAT freezes the trained modules and learns layer-wise weights, so it is a \(\oplus\) of \(k\) frozen line methods \(\alpha_i\mapsto\alpha_iB_iA_i\). Its per-layer image is a span of dimension at most \(k\), much smaller than \(\mathrm{LoRA}_{kr}\). UniPELT and MAM are not \(\oplus\)-words in \(\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0)\). Their adapter and prefix parts live on extensions (Exposé VI), and UniPELT's gates depend on the input.

The obstruction in the observation is the reuse of one pointing, since stacking the same \(A_0\) twice gives a rank-deficient frame. For \(r\ne s\), standard pointings satisfy \(F(r)\oplus F(s)\cong F(r+s)\) exactly when the row space of the rank-\((r+s)\) frame is the direct sum of the other two. Concatenation is strictly associative, so discrete \((\mathbb N,+)\) suffices for a plain strong monoidal functor on unpointed objects. Symmetric strong monoidality needs the permutation groupoid, since swapping and then concatenating permutes the blocks.

“Additive” in Def I.2 (\(\delta\) independent of \(\theta_0\)) is a condition on families \(\theta_0\mapsto M(\theta_0)\). It matters for base change (Exposé V) and plays no role in \(\oplus\).

With scales \(s_r=\alpha/r\) (or \(\alpha/\sqrt r\) for rsLoRA) and any target scale \(s_*\), the rescaled concatenation \(([B_1\ B_2],[c_1A_1;c_2A_2])\) with \(c_i=s_{r_i}/s_*\), where \(r_1=r\) and \(r_2=s\), is an isomorphism over \(\Theta\). It preserves row spaces, so the pointing criterion is unchanged. Hugging Face PEFT's add_weighted_adapter(..., combination_type="cat") takes \(s_*=1\). It multiplies each \(A\) block by \(w_is_{r_i}\), leaves the \(B\) blocks alone, and sets the merged lora_alpha to \(r+s\), so the merged scale is \(1\). In PEFT releases up to v0.21.1 the merged adapter also inherits use_rslora from the first source. For rsLoRA sources its scale is then \((r+s)/\sqrt{r+s}=\sqrt{r+s}\), and the merged update is \(\sqrt{r+s}\) times too large. The main branch now sets use_rslora=False on the merged adapter.

Products

Freeze \(A\) at \(A_0\) and train \(B\), or freeze \(B\) at \(B_0\) and train \(A\). The product of these two one-sided methods is LoRA-XS.

Proposition I.8 (a product that produces a named method; an image-level meet). (a) Let \(A_0\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}\) have full row rank and \(B_0\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}\) full column rank. Define the one-sided methods \[\mathrm{FA}(A_0):B\mapsto W_0+BA_0,\qquad\mathrm{FB}(B_0):A\mapsto W_0+B_0A,\] both pointed at \(0\). Then \[\mathrm{FA}(A_0)\times\mathrm{FB}(B_0)\;\cong\;\big(\mathbb R^{r\times r},\ 0,\ R\mapsto W_0+B_0RA_0\big).\] The product supplies this sandwich for any frames. Assume \(\operatorname{rank}W_0\ge r\) (\(\sigma_r\gt0\)) and take the SVD frames \(B_0=U_r\), \(A_0=\Sigma_rV_r^\top\). The result is LoRA-XS pointed at \(R=0\).

(b) Let \(W_0\) have full row rank (so \(m\le n\)), and let \(\mathrm{LoRA}_r\) carry its standard pointing (\(B_0=0\), image \(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\)). The image-level meet of \(\mathrm{LoRA}_r\) with the output-scaling method \(\ell\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(\ell)W_0\) is \(\{\mathrm{diag}(\ell)W_0:\#\{i:\ell_i\neq1\}\le r\}\), that is, “rescale at most \(r\) output neurons”.

The LoRA-XS paper writes \(xW+xARB\) in row-vector convention with \(A=U_r\Sigma_r\) and \(B=V_r^\top\), so the isomorphism is \(R\mapsto\alpha R^\top\), and its initialisation \(R\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma=10^{-5}\) is a negligible pointing defect. The output scaling in (b) is the key/value part of (IA)3. On the FFN, (IA)3 scales the input of \(W_{\rm down}\), \(W\mapsto W\mathrm{diag}(\ell)\), and the mirror statement needs full column rank, or holds generically for \(r\lt d\).

(a) A point of the fibre product is a pair \((B,A)\) with \(BA_0=B_0A=:D\). Then \(\mathrm{row}\,D\subseteq\mathrm{row}\,A_0\) and \(\mathrm{col}\,D\subseteq\mathrm{col}\,B_0\), so \(D=B_0RA_0\) for a unique \(R\). Since \(A_0\) has full row rank, \(BA_0=B_0RA_0\) forces \(B=B_0R\), and likewise \(A=RA_0\). So the fibre product is the \(r^2\)-dimensional linear subspace \(\{(B_0R,RA_0)\}\), and \(R\mapsto(B_0R,RA_0)\) is a smooth bijection onto it with smooth inverse \((B,A)\mapsto B_0^+B\). Any pair of maps \((h_1,h_2):T\to Q_{\rm FA}\times Q_{\rm FB}\) that agree over \(\Theta\) factors through it via \(t\mapsto B_0^+h_1(t)\), so this is the product in \(\mathbf{Diff}\). If \(\operatorname{rank}W_0\lt r\), then \(\Sigma_rV_r^\top\) is rank-deficient and the LoRA-XS instance breaks.

(b) \(\mathrm{diag}(\ell)W_0-W_0=\mathrm{diag}(\ell-\mathbf 1)W_0\), and its rank is \(\#\{i:\ell_i\ne1\}\) because the rows of \(W_0\) are independent.∎

LoRA-XS is the largest method that factors through both frozen frames. At a split initialisation it is the overlap the split double-counts (Prop II.3), and it is LoRA-XS proper at a PiSSA initialisation, whose frames span the top-\(r\) singular subspaces of \(W_0\). The meet in (b) is only set-level. For \(n\ge2\) no product exists in \(\mathbf{Diff}\): its fibre product is singular at the origin \((b,a,\ell)=(0,0,\mathbf 1)\), where its branches cross, by the rank argument for the cross in Obs I.4(b). (For \(m=n=1\) the fibre product is a graph, and the product exists.) With a fixed support, the meet is (IA)3 masked to \(r\) neurons.

More: the shape of the meet in (b)

The meet is the union of the \(\binom mr\) coordinate \(r\)-planes “rescale at most \(r\) output neurons”. For \(r=1\) the fibre product contains the sheets \(\{b=0\}\) and \(\{a=0\}\), which cross at the origin. The standard base point, with \(b=0\) and \(a_0\ne0\), lies on a smooth sheet, so the singularity sits away from it.
