Exposé V

Base change: moving the point

Quantising and restarting move the point a method is attached to, and combining adapters builds one update from several. Each move comes down to one computation, of the defect a quantiser leaves, the monoid one cycle generates, or the gauge a combination rule must ignore.

Study each object over a base, so that the base can be changed. What survives the change is what the object really is.

after Grothendieck
In one minute
  • QLoRA is LoRA anchored at the quantised weight \(\kappa\theta_0\), so it misses \(\theta_0\) by the quantisation error \(e\), generically of full rank. LoftQ and QPiSSA are built to shrink this defect, by quantising and splitting in opposite orders. HF PEFT’s LoftQ initialiser ignores the LoRA scale \(s=\alpha/r\), so with its default single iteration it beats QLoRA in Frobenius norm only for \(0\lt s\lt2\).
  • With a fixed shape, merge-and-restart reaches exactly the monoid one cycle generates. ReLoRA reaches full rank after \(\lceil\min(m,n)/r\rceil\) cycles, while fixed linear shapes such as BitFit, (IA)\(^3\) or a fixed mask gain nothing.
  • Exactly orthogonal block OFT on a fixed partition never leaves \(W_0\cdot\prod SO(b)\), and re-merged BOFT reaches all rotations only at full butterfly depth. HF’s default Cayley–Neumann OFT is not orthogonal, and with small generators each merge can only shrink neuron norms and singular values.
  • A rule combining LoRA factors is well defined only if it ignores each adapter’s \(GL_r\) gauge. Task arithmetic on the products passes. LoraHub and HF’s factor-wise linear merge fail through cross terms, and become exact only when the adapters share a factor.

The point moves

Every method so far has been an arrow into one fixed point \((\Theta,\theta_0)\). In practice the point moves. Quantisation moves it once, ReLoRA at every merge, and task arithmetic, LoraHub, TIES and DARE build a new update from several adapters. A PEFT method is a formula that makes sense over any base point, so it is a family \(\theta_0\mapsto M(\theta_0)\), and this exposé relates its members. Four kinds of move occur.

Definition V.1 (base change and rebasing). (a) A function-preserving pointed reparametrisation \(\phi:(\Theta',f',\theta_0')\to(\Theta,f,\theta_0)\), with \(f\circ(\phi\times X)=f'\), induces \[\phi_!:\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta',\theta_0')\to\mathsf{Meth}(\Theta,\theta_0),\qquad(Q,q_0,\rho)\mapsto(Q,q_0,\phi\circ\rho),\] by postcomposition.

(b) A map \(\kappa:\Theta\to\Theta\) that does not preserve the function, such as a quantiser, moves the base point. Its pointing defect is \(e=\kappa\theta_0-\theta_0\).

(c) A rebasing scheme (merge-and-restart) picks \(\theta_{k+1}\in\mathrm{Im}\,M(\theta_k)\). Its reachable sets are \(R_K\), the points \(\theta_K\) it can reach in \(K\) cycles, and \(R_\infty=\bigcup_KR_K\).

Only part (a) is base change in the algebraic geometer’s sense, and it costs nothing; whether \(\phi_!\) turns LoRA into LoRA is the covariance question of Observation II.15. Parts (b) and (c) borrow the word, and the work lies there and in the combination rules. Figure 11 lets you run the restart computations of Theorem V.3 by hand.

More: what “base change” means here

In algebraic geometry a family over a base \(S\) pulls back along any map \(S'\to S\), and the statements worth having are the ones that survive the change. Part (a) of Definition V.1 is the standard companion of pullback on slice categories, composition with a map of bases. A quantiser changes the network’s function, so there is no commuting square to transport along, and we compute the defect it leaves instead. A rebasing scheme is an iteration, and we compute its reachable set. “Gauge” keeps the sense of Exposé III, the symmetry group of a parametrisation; no connection is involved.

Quantisation and splitting

For an additive method (Def I.2) whose starting point does not depend on the base, such as zero-init LoRA, quantising the base only translates the image, so QLoRA is LoRA with its vertex at \(\kappa\theta_0\). A quantisation error is spread over all singular directions, so \(e\) is generically of full rank and the image misses \(\theta_0\). Methods whose starting point is computed from the base, such as PiSSA, CorDA, OLoRA, LoftQ and DoRA, meet two operations that do not commute, and the order fixes the defect (tabulated in Exposé II.A).

2026SRR (Cho et al., 2026) joins the first two orders of Proposition V.2(b) by splitting the rank budget. It puts \(\mathrm{SVD}_k(W)\) into the adapter, quantises \(W_{\rm res}=W-\mathrm{SVD}_k(W)\) and fits the quantisation error with the other \(r-k\) ranks, so its defect is \(e_k\) minus its best rank-\((r-k)\) approximation, where \(e_k=\kappa(W_{\rm res})-W_{\rm res}\), and \(\mathrm{SVD}_k\) and best approximations are taken in its activation-scaled norm. With that scaling set to the identity, \(k=r\) gives the split-first defect of QPiSSA and \(k=0\) that of one-step LoftQ at \(s=1\). SRR picks \(k\) per matrix from a closed-form proxy.
Proposition V.2 (quantisation and splitting). Write \(W\) for the full-precision weight, \(\kappa\) for the quantiser, \(e=\kappa W-W\), \(\delta_0=\delta(q_0)\), and \(s\) for the LoRA scale \(\alpha/r\) (\(\alpha/\sqrt r\) under rsLoRA).

(a) Additive families commute with translation, \(M(\theta+v)=\tau_v\circ M(\theta)\). Hence QLoRA is LoRA pointed at \(\kappa\theta_0\), with defect \(e\), and \(\theta_0\notin\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{LoRA}_r(\kappa\theta_0)\) unless \(\operatorname{rank}e\le r\).

(b) For splitting families derived from an SVD or from data, three orders occur in practice.

  • Split, then quantise the residual (QPiSSA; HF’s PiSSA and CorDA workflow; LoftQ’s quantisation half-steps for \(t\ge2\)). The defect is \(\kappa(W_{\rm res})-W_{\rm res}\).
  • Quantise, then fit the error (one-step LoftQ, HF’s default loftq_iter=1; HF replace_lora_weights_loftq). HF PEFT does not divide by \(s\), so \(\delta_0=-s\,\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\), and the defect \(e-s\,\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\) has squared norm \(\|e-\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\|^2+(1-s)^2\|\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\|^2\). At \(s=1\), HF’s bare default \(r=\alpha=8\), this is \(e\) minus its best rank-\(r\) approximation; at \(s=2\) its norm is \(\|e\|\).
  • Quantise, split, re-quantise (HF olora_init on bitsandbytes weights). The defect is \(e+\kappa(r')-r'\), with \(r'=\kappa W-\delta_0\).

Quantise-then-split has defect exactly \(e\) iff the frozen residual equals \(\kappa W-\delta_0\), for instance \(\kappa W\) minus a frozen copy of \(\delta_0\). Action families with a base-independent pointing (OFT at \(R=I\), BOFT at \(R=I\) with unit scale, HiRA at \(B=0\)) carry the defect \(e\). DoRA’s magnitude gives defect \(e\) when initialised from \(\kappa\theta_0\), and \(\mathrm{diag}\big(\|w_{0,i}\|/\|(\kappa\theta_0)_i\|\big)\,\kappa\theta_0-\theta_0\) when initialised from \(\theta_0\). For action families the absolute invariants move too. NF4’s exact zero code enlarges the zero set HiRA must preserve, and exactly orthogonal OFT preserves the Gram matrix of \(\kappa\theta_0\) rather than that of \(\theta_0\).

(c) LoftQ alternates an exact rank-\(r\) SVD step with round-to-nearest quantisation whose blockwise scales are recomputed from its input. The second step is not an exact projection, so the objective \(\|\theta_0-Q-\delta(q)\|\) need not decrease monotonically. At \(s=1\) the output is an approximate section, a choice \((Q,q_0)\) with reduced \(\|\rho(q_0)-\theta_0\|\). In general, with \(Q_T\) and the low-rank fit \(L_TR_T\) after \(T\) iterations, the deployed defect is \((Q_T+L_TR_T-W)+(s-1)L_TR_T\).

(d) For a generic high-precision adapter \(\Delta\), fine-tune-then-quantise and quantise-then-fine-tune do not commute, since \(\kappa\theta_0+\Delta\) leaves the quantisation grid. For a uniform grid with fixed scales the obstruction is the off-grid update; NF4, and absmax grids with recomputed scales, are not closed under addition at all. Type-preserving merges, such as HF PEFT’s dequantise–add–requantise for bitsandbytes layers, stay in the quantised type but are lossy. Under affine group quantisation QA-LoRA is the structured exception. Its group-constant adapter merges into the zero-points, so the merged model stays INT-\(k\).

For the last clause, \(\kappa\theta_0+\delta(q)=\theta_0\) needs \(\delta(q)=-e\), and LoRA’s \(\delta(q)\) has rank at most \(r\).

(b) Compute \(\rho(q_0)-W\) in each pipeline. Splitting first gives \(\kappa(W_{\rm res})+\delta_0-W\) with \(W_{\rm res}=W-\delta_0\) (for LoftQ, \(W_{\rm res}=W-\delta(q_{t-1})\)). Fitting the error gives \(\kappa W-s\,\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)-W=(e-\mathrm{SVD}_r(e))+(1-s)\,\mathrm{SVD}_r(e)\), and the two terms are orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product. Re-quantising gives \(\kappa(r')+\delta_0-W=(\kappa(r')-r')+(\kappa W-W)\). For DoRA at \(B=0\), \(\rho(q_0)=\mathrm{diag}\big(\mu_0/\|(\kappa\theta_0)_i\|\big)\,\kappa\theta_0\), where \(\mu_0\) is the vector of row norms of whichever base initialised the magnitude.

(c) holds by construction, with deployed weight \(Q_T+s\,L_TR_T\); the failure of monotonicity is observed numerically. (d) In general \(\kappa(\theta_0+\Delta)\ne\kappa\theta_0+\Delta\). On a quantisation group with codes \(c\), scale \(a\) and zero-point \(\beta\), a group-constant \(\Delta\) gives \(ac+\beta+\Delta=ac+(\beta+\Delta)\), so it is absorbed into the zero-point.

Restarting: what merge-and-restart can reach

Merge the adapter, restart at the merged point, repeat. Each cycle adds one more copy of an additive shape or applies one more group element, so the long-run reach is whatever one cycle generates. A rank-\(r\) cone generates every matrix, and a subspace, subgroup or submonoid generates only itself. Block-orthogonal factors generate the rotations of each connected piece of their block hypergraph, because the bracket of infinitesimal rotations in the planes \((a,c)\) and \((c,b)\) is the infinitesimal rotation in the plane \((a,b)\).

Theorem V.3 (rebasing closure). (a) Additive methods with a fixed shape \(C\ni0\), with frames, masks and compression operators frozen at \(\theta_0\) across cycles. Then \(R_K=\theta_0+C^{+K}\), the \(K\)-fold Minkowski sum, and \(R_\infty=\theta_0+\mathrm{Mon}(C)\), the additive monoid generated by \(C\). If \(C\) is closed under real scalings, \(R_\infty=\theta_0+\mathrm{span}\,C\).

(b) Action methods, where one cycle applies an element of a set \(S\) with \(1\in S\subseteq G\); for scaling methods, \(S\subseteq\mathrm{End}(\Theta)\) is a subset containing the identity. Then \(R_K=S^K\theta_0\), and \(R_\infty\) is the orbit of the monoid generated by \(S\). Suppose \(S\) lies in the subgroup generated by connected Lie subgroups \(H_1,\dots,H_p\) and contains a neighbourhood of \(1\) in each. Then the monoid generated by \(S\) is the connected Lie subgroup \(H\) with \(\mathrm{Lie}(H)=\mathrm{Lie}\langle\mathfrak h_1,\dots,\mathfrak h_p\rangle\).

(c) Block-orthogonal factors. Let the factors have coordinate blocks \(I_j\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\) (block OFT, BOFT factors, Givens planes). Then \[\mathrm{Lie}\langle\mathfrak{so}(I_j)\rangle_j=\bigoplus_C\mathfrak{so}(C),\] summed over the connected components \(C\) of the hypergraph \((\{1,\dots,n\},\{I_j\})\).

Corollaries (of Theorem V.3). The following hold for the named methods under rebasing.
  1. ReLoRA. \(R_K=W_0+\mathcal M_{\le\min(Kr,m,n)}\). It reaches full fine-tuning iff \(K\ge\lceil\min(m,n)/r\rceil\).
  2. Idempotent methods gain nothing from rebasing. Their shape is exactly a subspace, subgroup or submonoid independent of the base, with frames, masks and compression operators frozen across cycles. Examples are LoRA-FA with a fixed \(A\), LoRA-XS with frozen frames, MoRA with fixed operators, FourierFT with fixed indices, WaveFT, C3A, MiSS in its default variant, SHiRA, BitFit, FISH with a fixed mask, LN-tuning, (IA)\(^3\), SSF and RoAd. Block OFT with a fixed partition and an exact Cayley map gains only the measure-zero set of block rotations with an eigenvalue \(-1\), so \(R_\infty=W_0\cdot\prod SO(b)=\overline{R_1}\); COFT (an \(\varepsilon\)-ball) with an exact Cayley map gains up to that set. Methods that recompute frames or masks from the merged weight (an SVD-recomputing LoRA-XS, ReMoRA’s alternating operators, resampled indices) do gain.
  3. VeRA. \(\mathrm{VeRA}^\infty=\text{LoRA-FA}(A)\) whenever the frozen \(B\) has no zero entries.
  4. OFT family. Block OFT with an exact Cayley map is stuck in \(W_0\cdot\prod SO(b)\). HF’s default Cayley–Neumann factor is \(R=C(Q)(I-Q^4)\), with \(C\) the exact Cayley map, so \(R^\top R=(I-Q^4)^2\) and it is not orthogonal. Its singular values are \(\lvert1-\mu_k^4\rvert\), where \(\pm i\mu_k\) are the eigenvalues of \(Q\). When every \(\mu_k\le2^{1/4}\), the regime the truncated series is meant for, each factor is a contraction, so each merge can only shrink \(K_{\rm out}\) in the Loewner order, and no singular value grows. Iterated BOFT (exact Cayley in HF) with \(m'\) butterfly factors of block size \(b\) reaches \[\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb R^m)\cdot W_0\cdot P\Big(\prod SO\big(b\,2^{m'-1}\big)\Big)P^\top,\] because the factor hypergraph has components of size \(b\,2^{m'-1}\). For injective \(W_0\) this is \(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb R^m)\cdot W_0\cdot SO(n)\) iff \(b\,2^{m'-1}=n\), the full-depth butterfly \(m'=1+\log_2(n/b)\). HF’s default boft_n_butterfly_factor=1 is block OFT with an output scale. A single full-depth BOFT has dimension at most \(m'\tfrac nb\binom b2\), below \(\dim SO(n)\) when \(m'(b-1)\lt n-1\).
  5. Re-HRA (HF apply_GS=False), with \(W_0\) injective and \(r\) even. \(R_K=(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le Kr})\cap W_0\cdot SO(n)\), so rebasing commutes with the meet of Theorem II.7. This multiplicative twin of ReLoRA reaches \(W_0\cdot SO(n)\) in at most \(\lceil n/r\rceil\) cycles.
  6. GaLore (with a gradient-SVD or a random projector), LISA and MeZO, under the optimizer hypotheses of Prop IV.2, are rebasing schemes of linear methods whose shape moves. As a set, over the sequences of shapes they can choose, they reach all of \(\Theta\), while a fixed linear shape \(L\) reaches only \(\theta_0+L\). Low rank is the per-period obstruction only for GaLore; LISA and MeZO already make full-rank per-matrix updates. Flora as published takes full-space steps with a compressed momentum, so it has no per-period low-rank shape.
  7. Merging never rescues an exactly orthogonal method from its absolute invariants. Every exactly orthogonal input-side rebasing preserves \(K_{\rm out}\) and the spectrum (Cor II.6); output-side ones preserve \(K_{\rm in}\) and the spectrum.

(a) follows by induction on \(K\). If \(C=\mathbb RC\), the monoid generated by \(C\) contains \(-C\) and is closed under scaling, so it is the span.

(b) Each cycle applies one element of \(S\), so \(R_K=S^K\theta_0\), and since \(1\in S\) the sets increase with \(K\). Any neighbourhood of \(1\) contains a symmetric one, and a symmetric neighbourhood of \(1\) in a connected group generates that group as a monoid. The subgroup generated by connected Lie subgroups is the connected Lie subgroup with the generated Lie algebra (Yamabe).

(c) Write \(L_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}\). Then \([L_{ac},L_{cb}]=L_{ab}\) for distinct \(a,b,c\). Overlapping hyperedges therefore generate \(\mathfrak{so}\) of their union, and \(\bigoplus_C\mathfrak{so}(C)\) is a subalgebra that contains every generator.

The corollaries.

  • (1) Rank is subadditive, and an SVD splits into blocks of rank at most \(r\).
  • (2) The exact Cayley image is dense and open in \(SO(b)\), and every element of \(SO(b)\) is a product of two elements of it.
  • (3) The entries of \(\Lambda_bB\Lambda_d\) are \(b_iB_{ij}d_j\). Their span over \((b,d)\) is all of \(\mathbb R^{m\times r}\) when every \(B_{ij}\ne0\) (take \(b=e_i\), \(d=e_j\)).
  • (4) Apply (c) to the butterfly blocks. For the Cayley–Neumann factors, \((I-Q)(I+Q+Q^2+Q^3)=I-Q^4\) gives \(R=C(Q)(I-Q^4)\), where \(C(Q)\) is orthogonal and commutes with the symmetric matrix \(Q^4\). Hence the singular values of \(R\) are \(\lvert1-\mu_k^4\rvert\), at most \(1\) when every \(\mu_k\le2^{1/4}\), and \(K_{\rm out}(WR^\top)=W(I-Q^4)^2W^\top\preceq WW^\top\). Also \(\det R=\prod_k(1-\mu_k^4)^2\ge0\), and \(R\) is singular when some \(\mu_k=1\). The invertible factors generate \(\prod GL^+(b)\) as a group (\(\prod CO^+(2)\) when \(b=2\)): \(R(Q)R(-Q)=(I-Q^4)^2\) gives every positive scaling that is constant on the rotation planes of some skew \(Q\), \(R(Q)\lvert I-Q^4\rvert^{-1}=C(Q)\) for small \(Q\) gives \(SO(b)\), and \(GL^+(b)=SO(b)\cdot D^+\cdot SO(b)\); for \(b=2\) the scalings are scalar. This identifies the group only. Re-merging reaches the monoid, which can be smaller, as the contraction bound shows.
  • (5) “\(\subseteq\)” holds because \(R_1R_2-I=R_1(R_2-I)+(R_1-I)\), so ranks add. For “\(\supseteq\)”, an \(R\in SO(n)\) with \(k=\operatorname{rank}(R-I)\le Kr\) is a product of exactly \(k\) reflections, with \(k\) even (Cartan–Dieudonné in Scherk’s form). Split them into \(K\) consecutive groups of even size at most \(r\), pad each group with pairs \(H_uH_u=I\), and observe that each group is an \(\mathrm{HRA}_r\) element. Injectivity of \(W_0\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}W_0(R-I)=\operatorname{rank}(R-I)\).
  • (6) A fixed linear shape \(L\) is its own additive monoid, by corollary 2.
  • The remaining corollaries are immediate.

Prediction Re-merged exact-Cayley block OFT and COFT on a fixed partition never leave \(W_0\cdot\prod SO(b)\); going further needs partitions that change between cycles or full-depth butterflies. These are statements about reach, and whether extra reach improves a trained model is the experiment.

Combining: rules that must ignore the gauge

A LoRA module ships as factors \((B,A)\), the model sees only \(BA\), and \((Bg^{-1},gA)\) gives the same product for every \(g\in GL_r\). A combination rule should return the same model for every choice of factors, or two people with the same adapters can merge them into different models. Task arithmetic adds the products and passes. LoraHub adds the \(B\)’s, adds the \(A\)’s and multiplies, and fails, because its cross terms \(B_iA_l\) see a rescaling \((cB,A/c)\) of one module. Tab B of Figure 9 shows the LoraHub merge moving under that rescaling while task arithmetic stays fixed.

Proposition V.4 (combination rules: gauge and naturality). (a) A rule \(\Phi\big((B_i,A_i)_i\big)\) for combining LoRA factors descends to a function of the adapters \((B_iA_i)_i\) iff it is constant on the fibres of \((B_i,A_i)\mapsto(B_iA_i)\). On the full-rank locus this is equivalent to \(\prod_iGL_r\)-invariance. It is also equivalent for \(\Phi\) continuous on the whole factor space; this clause rests on the unique closed orbit in each fibre, which lies in the closure of every orbit of the fibre.
  • Task arithmetic \(\sum_iw_is_iB_iA_i\) (HF’s cat), with \(s_i\) the LoRA scale \(\alpha_i/r_i\), or \(\alpha_i/\sqrt{r_i}\) under rsLoRA, is invariant and lands in \(\mathrm{LoRA}_{\sum_ir_i}\).
  • LoraHub’s factor-wise rule \(\big(\sum_iw_iB_i\big)\big(\sum_jw_jA_j\big)\) is not invariant. Replacing \((B_2,A_2)\) by \((cB_2,A_2/c)\) changes it by \[\sum_{l\ne2}w_lw_2\big[(c-1)B_2A_l+(c^{-1}-1)B_lA_2\big].\] HF’s add_weighted_adapter(..., combination_type="linear") is a factor-wise rule of the same type. HF’s non-SVD ties, dare_* and magnitude_prune variants also act on factors; the *_svd variants act on full updates.
  • Poly and MHR train their skill inventories jointly through the product-of-sums map. Its evident symmetry, one common \(g\in GL_r\) for every skill, leaves every combined adapter unchanged, so their cross terms are a design choice of a jointly trained parametrisation.

(b) Well-definedness versus exactness. A joint gauge fixing makes a factor-wise rule well defined. Exactness, agreement with task arithmetic for all weights, needs degree one in \(w\), which for product-of-sums rules with injective factors means a shared factor. A frozen shared factor (FFA-LoRA; LoRA-FA) gives \(\big(\sum_iw_iB_i\big)A=\sum_iw_iB_iA\). LoraHub with a shared \(A\) returns \(\big(\sum_jw_j\big)\sum_iw_iB_iA\), exact up to that unnormalised factor. A per-module SVD still leaves a \((\mathbb Z/2)^r\) of sign flips per module, plus rotations within repeated singular values, and these change the cross terms. A joint re-factorisation with a shared factor, such as KnOTS’s shared \(U\Sigma\), does not. FedAvg-LoRA clients start each round from the same broadcast \((B,A)\), so their failure, \[\tfrac12(B_1+B_2)\cdot\tfrac12(A_1+A_2)\ne\tfrac12(B_1A_1+B_2A_2),\] is bilinearity, and it persists in a fully fixed gauge.

(c) Naturality. On updates with \(N\) coordinates, write \(T_N\), \(B_N\) and \(\mathrm{Mon}_N\) for the invertible diagonal, signed permutation and monomial matrices. Task arithmetic is \(GL(\Theta)\)-equivariant. DARE satisfies \(D(m\odot\tau)=m\odot(D\tau)\) for every invertible diagonal \(D\) and the same mask \(m\), so it is exactly \(T_N\)-equivariant per mask, sign flips included, and \(\mathrm{Mon}_N\)-equivariant in distribution. TIES with global trimming and sign election by total mass (the paper’s rule and HF’s default) is equivariant under \(\mathbb R^\times\cdot B_N\), homotheties times signed permutations, away from ties in magnitude. None of these sparsifying rules is \(O(N)\)-equivariant. Applied to factors and summed across adapters, as in HF’s non-SVD variants, they are gauge-dependent through the cross terms of (a). Factor-wise DARE is invariant under one common \(g\in\mathrm{Mon}_r\) applied to every adapter, exactly for the torus with the same masks and in law for permutations, while a gauge on a single adapter changes its cross terms.

(a) On the full-rank locus, the fibres of \((B_i,A_i)\mapsto(B_iA_i)\) are \(\prod GL_r\)-orbits (Thm II.2). Off it, invariance alone is not enough. The projector onto \(\mathrm{row}\,A\) is \(GL_r\)-invariant on \(\{\operatorname{rank}A=r\}\), yet it differs at \((0,A)\) and \((0,A')\), which have the same product. For a continuous \(\Phi\), invariance makes \(\Phi\) constant on orbit closures, and the unique closed orbit of a fibre lies in the closure of every orbit of that fibre. The LoraHub formula follows by expanding the product: the diagonal term \(w_2^2B_2A_2\) is unchanged, and each cross term \(w_lw_2B_2A_l\) gains a factor \(c\) while \(w_lw_2B_lA_2\) gains \(c^{-1}\). For Poly and MHR, a common \(g\) cancels inside every product of sums. Whether it is the only continuous symmetry depends on the routing: with at least as many skills as tasks, or with the routing counted as a parameter, there are further ones, such as relabelling the skills.

(b) Flipping column \(j\) of \(B_i\) together with row \(j\) of \(A_i\) preserves the SVD form and changes \(B_iA_l\) for \(l\ne i\). A product of two weighted sums is quadratic in \(w\), unless one factor is shared and left unweighted.

(c) Masks and the rescaling by \(1/(1-p)\) commute with diagonal maps, and an i.i.d. mask is permutation-invariant in law. Trimming by magnitude, electing signs by total mass and taking disjoint means commute with signed coordinate permutations and with \(c\cdot I\) for \(c\ne0\). A rotation changes the magnitudes of individual coordinates. For the factor clause, the all-ones mask has positive probability, and on that event factor-wise DARE is the product of sums \(\big(\sum_iw_iB_i\big)\big(\sum_jw_jA_j\big)/(1-p)^2\), which a gauge \((cB_2,A_2/c)\) on one adapter changes by the formula of (a). A common diagonal gauge commutes with the masks and cancels in every product.

Num DARE with a random non-unit diagonal \(D\) is equivariant to \(2\times10^{-15}\) per mask. TIES is homogeneous to \(9\times10^{-16}\) and fails by \(5.6\) under a generic positive diagonal. The LoraHub change formula matches with three modules to \(2\times10^{-15}\). On the factors of two adapters, a diagonal gauge on one of them moves factor-wise DARE by an amount of order one, while the same gauge shared by both leaves it fixed to \(10^{-15}\).

More: TIES variants and HF options

With per-tensor trimming, TIES is equivariant instead under \(\prod_b\big(\mathbb R^\times\cdot B_{N_b}\big)\), a signed permutation and a scalar per block, together with permutations of equal-size blocks. DARE followed by full TIES is \(\mathbb R^\times\cdot B_N\)-equivariant in distribution. HF’s dare_ties has no magnitude trim, so with total-mass election it is exactly \(T_N\)-equivariant per mask, like DARE. With HF’s majority_sign_method="frequency", sign counts are integers, ties are common and are broken towards \(+1\), so sign flips and negative homotheties fail for ties and dare_ties alike. HF’s linear rule weights \(A_i\) by \(\mathrm{sign}(w_i)\sqrt{\lvert w_is_i\rvert}\) and \(B_i\) by \(\sqrt{\lvert w_is_i\rvert}\), so even on adapters that share a frozen \(A\) it is not exact up to a global factor; the relative weights become \(\sqrt{\lvert w_is_i\rvert}\).

Prediction LoraHub behaves like update arithmetic, up to the factor \(\sum_jw_j\), when its modules share the \(A\)-seed and \(A\) stays close to its initialisation. That regime is empirical and favoured by a large ratio \(\eta_B/\eta_A\), as in LoRA+. LoraHub degrades when the modules come from independent seeds.

What base change leaves for the network

Every move here stayed inside weight space, where merging into the base is the canonical arrow to the terminal object of Observation I.4 and always exists. Adapters, prefixes and ReFT change the network itself, and for them merging is a lifting problem whose obstructions the activation function decides. That is Exposé VI.
