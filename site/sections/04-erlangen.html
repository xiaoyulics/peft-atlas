Exposé II.B

Erlangen: what a method can never change

A method that moves the pretrained weight by a group element can never change that group's invariants. Orbits also place HRA as a meet, DoRA as a saturation and the scaling methods on a ladder.

A geometry is what a group leaves unchanged. Before asking how a method learns, ask what it cannot help keeping.

after Klein
In one minute
  • A method whose weights are always \(W_0R\), with \(R\) exactly orthogonal, can never change \(WW^\top\), so neuron norms, angles and singular values survive any number of merges in exact arithmetic. HF PEFT's default OFT, BOFT's scale and GOFT's default scaler break this.
  • For injective \(W_0\) and even \(r\le n-2\), HRA reaches exactly the rank-\(r\) LoRA updates of the form \(W_0R\), \(R\in SO(n)\). Its HF PEFT default initialisation is an apex.
  • DoRA reaches exactly the weights of LoRA plus a free gain per output neuron. From the same start, any gap between the two comes from training.
  • (IA)³, SSF's scales and DoRA's magnitude lie in the image of rank-one HiRA, \(W_0\odot(J+BA)\), which for \(W_0\) without zeros is LoRA seen through \(X\mapsto W_0\odot X\).

Klein's Erlangen programme of 1872 defines a geometry by its group of motions and studies what the motions leave unchanged. OFT, GOFT, HRA and ETHER multiply the pretrained matrix by an orthogonal matrix, (IA)³ and SSF's scale multiply it by a diagonal one, and DoRA's magnitude does the same to a LoRA weight. These are methods of action type (Definition I.2), \(\rho(q)=\gamma(q)\cdot W_0\) with \(\gamma\) landing in a group. Whatever that group cannot change, the method cannot change, in training or after any number of merges.

CreditKlein's programme is the model. Bronstein et al., 2021 made it the organising principle of geometric deep learning, for architectures. Here it is applied to the fine-tuning map.
Analogy (what “Erlangen” means here). For a group-type method the Erlangen reading is a theorem, since its image lies in one orbit. LoRA's cone \(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\) has a singular vertex at \(W_0\) (Theorem II.2(c)), so it is no orbit; for cones the word names the shape of the image and its covariance group (Definition II.14).

One convention matters. A linear box computes \(y=Wx\) with \(W\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), and row \(i\) of \(W\) is output neuron \(i\). The two Gram matrices, over neurons and over inputs, are

\[K_{\rm out}(W)=WW^\top,\qquad K_{\rm in}(W)=W^\top W.\]

A right multiplication \(W\mapsto WR\) acts on the input side and fixes \(K_{\rm out}\); a left multiplication \(W\mapsto PW\) acts on the output side and fixes \(K_{\rm in}\). Papers that store \(W^\top\) swap these words, and we translate every method into this convention.

Complete invariants

A complete invariant of a group action agrees on two matrices exactly when one can be carried to the other. For the five actions in PEFT it is classical. We write \(T_m\) for the invertible diagonal matrices, \(T_m^+=\{\mathrm{diag}(d):d_i\gt0\}\) for its identity component, and \(\odot\) for the entrywise product.

Theorem II.5 (complete invariants of the basic actions). Let \(W,W'\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\).
  1. Right orthogonal (input side). \(W'=WR\) for some \(R\in O(n)\) iff \(WW^\top=W'W'^\top\). For \(SO(n)\), when \(\operatorname{rank}W=n\), also require that the then unique \(R\) have \(\det R=1\).
  2. Left orthogonal. \(W'=PW\) for some \(P\in O(m)\) iff \(W^\top W=W'^\top W'\).
  3. Two-sided. \(W'=PWR\) for some \(P\in O(m)\) and \(R\in O(n)\) iff \(W\) and \(W'\) have the same singular values, counted with multiplicity.
  4. Left torus. If \(W\) has no zero row, \(W'=DW\) for some \(D\in T_m^+\) iff \(w_i'\in\mathbb R_{\gt0}\,w_i\) for every \(i\). For the full torus \(T_m\), whose signs are unconstrained, the complete invariant is the set of zero rows together with the row lines.
  5. Full Hadamard torus. \(W'=E\odot W\) for some \(E\in(\mathbb R^\times)^{m\times n}\) iff \(W\) and \(W'\) have the same zero pattern.
(expand)

(1) If all inner products of rows agree, the map \(\sum_ic_iw_i\mapsto\sum_ic_iw_i'\) is well defined and isometric on the row spans, because \(\|\sum_ic_iw_i\|^2=c^\top WW^\top c\). The two row spans have the same dimension, \(\operatorname{rank}WW^\top\). Extend the map by an isometry of the orthogonal complements to \(R^\top\in O(n)\); then \(W'=WR\). The converse is immediate.

(2) is (1) applied to transposes. (3) follows from the singular value decomposition. (4) and (5) hold entry by entry; for (5) put \(E_{ij}=W'_{ij}/W_{ij}\) on the support and \(1\) off it. ∎

More: where Theorem II.5 comes from

Item (1) follows from the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for \(O(n)\), or from Witt's extension of isometries. LoHa's gauge in Proposition II.12, the two-sided diagonal torus \(\{\mathrm{diag}(a)\,X\,\mathrm{diag}(b)\}\), differs from the full Hadamard torus in (5).

No PEFT method realises (4) or (5) as a group action. (IA)³ gains and DoRA magnitudes are unconstrained reals, and HiRA's factor \(J+BA\) can vanish entrywise. These methods can create new zeros, so only the one-way statement transfers, that the zeros of \(W_0\) remain zero.

What exactly orthogonal methods can never do

If every weight a method reaches is \(W_0R\) with \(R\) orthogonal, Theorem II.5(1) and (3) freeze the neuron Gram matrix and the singular values, and merging cannot help, since products of orthogonal matrices are orthogonal. The work lies in saying which implementations are exactly orthogonal, and on which side.

Corollary II.6 (what exactly orthogonal methods can never do). Let a method have image in \(W_0\cdot O(n)\) (input side). Examples are OFT and block OFT with an exactly orthogonal parametrisation (the paper's exact Cayley map; HF PEFT with use_cayley_neumann=False, on linear and convolutional layers), GOFT as in its paper (official code with no_scaling=True), HRA, and ETHER as defined in its paper (not ETHER+). Then, in exact arithmetic and through any number of merge-and-restart cycles:
  • it preserves \(K_{\rm out}\), hence every neuron norm, every angle between nonzero neurons, and the “hyperspherical energy”, a function of these angles;
  • it preserves the whole singular spectrum of \(W_0\);
  • block-diagonal OFT with input blocks \(J\) and the same partition in every cycle preserves each partial Gram matrix \(W_{:,J}W_{:,J}^\top\).

Several methods that look orthogonal fail the hypothesis. Each row below is a separate computation.

Use Theorem II.5(1) and (3), and the fact that a product of orthogonal factors is orthogonal, so the image of any number of merge cycles stays in \(W_0\cdot O(n)\). For block-diagonal \(R\), \((WR)_{:,J}=W_{:,J}R_J\), which gives the partial Grams when the partition is fixed.

For BOFT, \(K_{\rm out}(\mathrm{diag}(s)W_0R^\top)=\mathrm{diag}(s)K_{\rm out}(W_0)\mathrm{diag}(s)\), so \(\cos'_{ij}=\mathrm{sign}(s_is_j)\cos_{ij}\) whenever \(s_is_j\ne0\). All cosines therefore survive when the \(s_i\) are nonzero and share one sign, and when no cosine of \(W_0\) vanishes this is also necessary. For GOFT's default code, \(K_{\rm out}(W_0\mathrm{diag}(g)G)=W_0\mathrm{diag}(g)GG^\top\mathrm{diag}(g)W_0^\top=W_0\mathrm{diag}(g)^2W_0^\top\).

For the Cayley–Neumann map, \((I-Q)(I+Q+Q^2+Q^3)=I-Q^4\), so \(R=(I+Q)(I+Q+Q^2+Q^3)=C(Q)(I-Q^4)\) with \(C(Q)=(I+Q)(I-Q)^{-1}\), which is HF's exact map \((I-Q)(I+Q)^{-1}\) evaluated at \(-Q\). \(C(Q)\) is orthogonal and commutes with \(Q^4\), and \((Q^4)^\top=Q^4\) for skew \(Q\). Hence \(R^\top R=(I-Q^4)^2\), and if the eigenvalues of \(Q\) are \(\pm i\theta_k\), the singular values of \(R\) are \(\lvert1-\theta_k^4\rvert\): at most \(1\) when every \(\theta_k\le2^{1/4}\), and \(0\) when some \(\theta_k=1\). With last coefficient \(2\), as in PEFT 0.16–0.17, the same computation gives \(R^\top R=I+4Q^6+4Q^8\). ∎

Numerical check: with \(s=(-0.5,1,\dots,1)\), BOFT changes a cosine by \(1.5\) while \(\lvert\cos\rvert\) is fixed to \(4\times10^{-16}\), and with every \(s_i\lt0\) the cosines are fixed to \(4\times10^{-16}\). HF's default OFT satisfies \(R^\top R=(I-Q^4)^2\) to \(5\times10^{-16}\), with \(\lVert R^\top R-I\rVert=1.5\times10^{-2}\) already for skew entries of size about \(0.07\) at block size \(8\). A conformal block \(\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Rot}(0.3)\) on two of \(n=8\) inputs changes some cosine by \(0.17\).

HRA is a meet of two images

A Householder reflection \(H_u=I-2uu^\top/\|u\|^2\) is orthogonal and differs from \(I\) by rank one. So \(W_0H_{u_1}\cdots H_{u_r}\) lies in the orthogonal orbit of \(W_0\) and in LoRA's cone \(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\), and for injective \(W_0\) and even \(r\) the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem says that HRA fills the cone's intersection with the rotation orbit. HF PEFT repeats each reflection at initialisation, so to first order each pair can only turn its own vector against the rest of the space, and HRA starts at an apex, as zero-init LoRA does (Theorem II.2).

Theorem II.7 (HRA is the meet of LoRA with the rotation orbit, and it starts at an apex). Let \(W_0\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) be injective (\(\operatorname{rank}W_0=n\)), and let \(r\) be even with \(2\le r\le n-2\). Define \(\mathrm{HRA}_r:(u_1,\dots,u_r)\mapsto W_0H_{u_1}\cdots H_{u_r}\) with \(u_i\ne0\), where \(H_u=I-2uu^\top/\|u\|^2\). Write \(\mathrm{OFT}_{SO}:=W_0\cdot SO(n)\) for the rotation orbit.
  1. Meet. \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r=(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})\cap W_0\cdot SO(n)\); the \(O(n)\)-orbit would add odd products such as \(W_0H_u\). Further, \(d(\mathrm{HRA}_r)=rn-\tfrac{r(r+1)}2\), so the generic fibre has dimension \(\tfrac{r(r+1)}2=rn-d\), and this gauge is non-linear.
  2. First-order collapse. At the paired initialisation \(u_{2i-1}=u_{2i}=w_i\) (\(i\le k=r/2\), with the \(w_i\) independent and \(U=\mathrm{span}\{w_i\}\)), \[S_1=W_0\cdot\{\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(n):P_{U^\perp}\Omega P_{U^\perp}=0\},\qquad\dim S_1=kn-\tfrac{k(k+1)}2\lt d(\mathrm{HRA}_r).\]
  3. Vertex. The germ of \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r\) at \(W_0\) is diffeomorphic to the germ at \(0\) of the skew-determinantal cone \(\Sigma_r=\{\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(n):\operatorname{rank}\Omega\le r\}\), which is not linear. So \(W_0\) is a singular point (the vertex) of the image. Together with (b), the paired initialisation is an apex.
  4. Scope.
    • Not a product. (a) is a meet in the poset of subsets of \(\Theta\) containing \(W_0\) (Observation I.4(c)), not a categorical product of \(\mathrm{LoRA}_r\) and \(\mathrm{OFT}_{SO}\). Over a generic point of the meet, the fibre product \(\{(B,A,R):W_0+BA=W_0R\}\) has local dimension \(d+r^2=rn+\tfrac{r(r-1)}2\ne rn\).
    • Exactly orthogonal OFT is smaller. Exact Cayley maps never produce a rotation with eigenvalue \(-1\). For exact full-block Cayley OFT, \((W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})\cap\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{OFT}\) is dense in \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r\) but misses \(W_0H_{e_1}H_{e_2}\), and for exact block OFT with \(b\lt n\) it is a proper subset. HF PEFT's default Cayley–Neumann OFT is not orthogonal (Corollary II.6); its meet with \(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}\) contains non-orthogonal points and is incomparable with \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r\).
    • Implementation. The theorem concerns HF's default HRA, without Gram–Schmidt (apply_GS=False); the paired initialisation is HF's default and is not stated in the HRA paper. HF HRA acts on in_features, so injectivity requires out_features \(\ge\) in_features, which excludes down_proj and GQA key/value projections.
(expand)

(a) “\(\subseteq\)”. Each \(H_u-I\) has rank one, so \(R=\prod_iH_{u_i}\) fixes \(\bigcap_iu_i^\perp\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(R-I)\le r\). Also \(\det R=(-1)^r=1\). Hence \(W_0R-W_0=W_0(R-I)\) has rank at most \(r\).

“\(\supseteq\)”. Suppose \(W_0R=W_0+X\) with \(\operatorname{rank}X\le r\) and \(R\in SO(n)\). Injectivity gives \(k':=\operatorname{rank}(R-I)\le r\). By the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem in Scherk's sharp form, \(R\) is a product of exactly \(k'\) reflections, and \(\det R=(-1)^{k'}\) forces \(k'\) to be even. Pad with \((r-k')/2\) pairs \(H_uH_u=I\). A single reflection has determinant \(-1\), so \(W_0H_u\), a rank-one update in the \(O(n)\)-orbit, is not in the image.

Dimension. A generic element is determined by its moved space \(\mathrm{im}(R-I)\in\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)\), of dimension \(r(n-r)\), together with a fixed-point-free element of \(SO(r)\) on it, an open set of dimension \(r(r-1)/2\). The sum is \(rn-r(r+1)/2\), and the generic fibre has dimension \(rn-d\). The identity \(H_aH_b=H_{H_ab}H_a\) shows that the Hurwitz move \((u_i,u_{i+1})\mapsto(H_{u_i}u_{i+1},u_i)\) preserves \(\rho\).

(b) Normalise \(\hat w=w/\|w\|\). A direct computation gives

\[\tfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\big(H_{w+\varepsilon a}H_{w+\varepsilon b}\big)\big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\tfrac{2}{\|w\|}\big(v\hat w^\top-\hat wv^\top\big),\qquad v=(I-\hat w\hat w^\top)(a-b).\]

Every pair equals \(I\) at \(q_0\), so the differential of the product is the sum of the pair differentials, and \(S_1=W_0\cdot\{\sum_iv_i\wedge\hat w_i\}\) with \(v\wedge w:=vw^\top-wv^\top\). This set lies in \(\{\Omega:P_{U^\perp}\Omega P_{U^\perp}=0\}\), because \(y^\top(v\hat w^\top-\hat wv^\top)x=0\) for \(x,y\perp\hat w\). Conversely, that space is spanned by the \(e_a\wedge e_b\) with \(e_a\in U\), and each is a combination of the elements \(\hat w_i\wedge e_b\). Its dimension is \(\dim\mathfrak{so}(n)-\dim\mathfrak{so}(n-k)=kn-k(k+1)/2\), and \(W_0\) is injective. Finally \(d-\dim S_1=k(2n-3k-1)/2\gt0\) for \(n\ge2k+2\).

(c) The Cayley map \(C(\Omega)=(I+\Omega)(I-\Omega)^{-1}\) is a diffeomorphism from a neighbourhood of \(0\in\mathfrak{so}(n)\) onto a neighbourhood of \(I\in SO(n)\), and \(C(\Omega)-I=2\Omega(I-\Omega)^{-1}\), so \(\operatorname{rank}(C(\Omega)-I)=\operatorname{rank}\Omega\). Composing with the linear embedding \(R\mapsto W_0R\) identifies the germs. \(\Sigma_r\) is a closed cone, and it is not linear, because a rank-\(r\) and a rank-\(2\) skew matrix can sum to rank \(r+2\le n\). A closed cone that is \(C^1\) at its vertex is linear, so \(0\) is singular. The apex statement uses (b); a singular image alone would not give a positive deficit.

(d) Over a generic point of the meet, the LoRA fibre is a \(GL_r\)-orbit and the \(SO(n)\) fibre is a point, since \(W_0\) is injective; this gives \(d+r^2\). Over \(W_0\) itself the fibre product contains \(\{(B,0,I)\}\), of dimension \(mr\), so it is not pure-dimensional. If \(C(\Omega)x=-x\), then \(y=(I-\Omega)^{-1}x\) satisfies \((I+\Omega)y=-(I-\Omega)y\), so \(y=0\). For density, perturb the angles \(\pi\) inside the same moved planes. For the Cayley–Neumann factor \(R\), a point \(W_0R\) of \(W_0\cdot O(n)\) needs \(R\) orthogonal, since \(W_0\) is injective, that is \((I-Q^4)^2=I\), which for small \(Q\) forces \(Q=0\). Conversely a skew \(Q\) of rank \(2\) inside one block gives \(\operatorname{rank}(R-I)=2\) with \(R\) not orthogonal, a point of LoRA \(\cap\) OFT outside \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r\). ∎

Numerical check: at \(n=9\) the paired-init Jacobian has rank \(8\) against generic rank \(15\) for \(r=2\), and \(15\) against \(26\) for \(r=4\).

More: symmetries, Gram–Schmidt and odd rank

The evident continuous symmetries of HRA's gauge are the \(r\) scalings \(u_i\mapsto c_iu_i\); the braid group also acts, by Hurwitz moves. HRA with Gram–Schmidt (apply_GS=True) has the smaller image \(W_0(I-2P_U)\), of dimension \(r(n-r)\), which never contains \(W_0\). For odd \(r\), HF falls back to a random initialisation, and \(\det R=(-1)^r\) shows that no odd-\(r\) HRA can start at \(W_0\).

Householder parametrisations in deep learning go back to Mhammedi et al., 2016, and Lie-algebra charts such as the Cayley map to Lezcano-Casado and Martínez-Rubio, 2019. Yuan et al., 2024 proposed HRA as a bridge between low-rank and orthogonal adaptation, which the theorem makes precise. The paired initialisation is read from the HF PEFT HRA layer.

HRA's “bridge between low-rank and orthogonal adaptation” is, at the level of images, the meet of LoRA with the rotation orbit. It strictly contains LoRA \(\cap\) OFT for exactly orthogonal OFT and is incomparable with it for HF's default OFT. Compare Proposition I.8(a), where LoRA-XS (Bałazy et al., 2024) is a genuine categorical product \(\mathrm{FA}\times\mathrm{FB}\). That HRA's default initialisation is an apex is new here. Its first-order collapse is the multiplicative twin of LoRA's dead \(A\)-direction (Lemma II.4), and whether it slows early training is open (Exposé XI). Rebasing commutes with the meet, so after \(K\) cycles re-HRA reaches \((W_0+\mathcal M_{\le Kr})\cap W_0\cdot SO(n)\) (Theorem V.3, corollary 5).

LOFT fixes one leaf of the meet

The proof of Theorem II.7 already shows how the meet is built. It is the union of the orbits \(W_0\cdot SO(V)\) over the \(r\)-dimensional input subspaces \(V\), one subgroup orbit for each point of the Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)\). Call each orbit a leaf. Leaves overlap along lower-rank strata, so this is a union and not a partition, but a generic point lies on exactly one, fixed by its moved space \(\mathrm{im}(R-I)\). HRA's reflection vectors move the leaf as it trains. LOFT (Zhao et al., 2026) freezes the leaf and makes the choice of leaf the design variable, which the paper calls the adaptation support.

2026One component of LoCO (Nguyen et al., 2026) is the Cayley rotation of a low-rank skew generator \(UV^\top-VU^\top\), with \(U,V\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}\). For injective \(W_0\) and \(2r\le n-2\), its image is \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_{2r}\) minus the points \(W_0R\) at which \(R\) has eigenvalue \(-1\), so like HRA it moves its leaf while it trains. Writing the generator as \(ZJZ^\top\) with \(Z=[U\ V]\) shows that its generic fibre is an orbit of the linear group \(Sp(2r,\mathbb R)\), acting by \(Z\mapsto ZM\). The non-linear gauge of Theorem II.7(a) therefore comes from the reflections, not from the meet. Its sum of several components is in general only approximately orthogonal, and then leaves the meet.
Example (LOFT: a leaf, and how to choose it). Let \(P_r\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}\) have orthonormal rows spanning \(V\), and let \(O(V)=\{I+P_r^\top(T-I)P_r:T\in O(r)\}\) be the orthogonal maps that fix \(V^\perp\) pointwise. LOFT trains \(\rho(E)=W_0\big(I+P_r^\top(Q(E)-I)P_r\big)\) with \(E\in\mathfrak{so}(r)\), \(E_0=0\), so \(|M|=r(r-1)/2\). We read it with an exactly orthogonal \(Q\) (the exponential, or the Cayley map \(C\)); the released code defaults to a five-term Cayley–Neumann series with \(R^\top R=(I-X^6)^2\), which is only approximately orthogonal, so like HF's default OFT (Corollary II.6) that version leaves its leaf.
  1. One leaf. If \(W_0P_r^\top\) has rank \(r\), then \(\big(W_0+W_0P_r^\top\,\mathbb R^{r\times r}P_r\big)\cap W_0\cdot O(n)=W_0\cdot O(V)\). For the principal support \(P_r=V_r^\top\) the first set is the image of LoRA-XS (this needs \(\sigma_r(W_0)\gt0\)), so the leaf is LoRA-XS \(\cap\,W_0\cdot O(n)\). Principal-support LOFT reaches an open dense part of its component \(W_0\cdot SO(V)\); for injective \(W_0\) and a Cayley \(Q\), its image is exactly LoRA-XS \(\cap\) the image of exact full-block Cayley OFT.
  2. Leaves over the Grassmannian. If \(W_0\) is injective, \((W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})\cap W_0\cdot SO(n)=\bigcup_{V\in\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)}W_0\cdot SO(V)\), which for even \(r\) is \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HRA}_r\) (Theorem II.7(a)). LOFT's image is the whole leaf \(W_0\cdot SO(V)\) with the exponential, and an open dense part of it with a Cayley map, which never reaches eigenvalue \(-1\).
  3. A regular base point. If \(W_0P_r^\top\) has rank \(r\), then \(S_1=W_0P_r^\top\mathfrak{so}(r)P_r\) has dimension \(r(r-1)/2=d(M)\): LOFT starts at a smooth point of its leaf and has no gauge, while HRA's paired initialisation is an apex (Theorem II.7(b)). For \(r\ge4\) there is no smooth pointed arrow LOFT\(\to\)HRA at that initialisation, although the images are nested (test O3).
  4. Choosing the leaf. If \(Q'(0)=\mathrm{id}\) (the exponential, or \(E\mapsto C(E/2)\)), the gradient pulled back to \(E\) at \(\theta_0\) is \(P_r\operatorname{skew}(W_0^\top G)P_r^\top\) with \(G=\nabla L(W_0)\); the Cayley map \(C\) itself doubles it, which does not change the best support. Its squared norm is at most \(2\sum_{k\le r/2}\mu_k^2\), where \(\pm i\mu_k\) are the eigenvalues of \(\operatorname{skew}(W_0^\top G)\), with equality when \(V\) spans the invariant subspace of the top \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\) pairs.
(expand)

(a) A rotation \(T\in O(r)\) gives \(W_0(I+P_r^\top(T-I)P_r)=W_0+W_0P_r^\top(T-I)P_r\), so “\(\supseteq\)” holds. Conversely let \(W=W_0+BXP_r\) with \(B=W_0P_r^\top\), and suppose \(W=W_0S\) with \(S\in O(n)\). Then \(WW^\top=W_0W_0^\top\), and expanding with \(P_rP_r^\top=I_r\) gives \(B\,(X+X^\top+XX^\top)\,B^\top=0\). Since \(B\) has full column rank, \(X+X^\top+XX^\top=0\), that is \((I+X)(I+X)^\top=I\), so \(T=I+X\in O(r)\). For \(P_r=V_r^\top\) we have \(B=U_r\Sigma_r\), and \(\{U_r\Sigma_rXV_r^\top\}=\{U_rR\,\Sigma_rV_r^\top\}\) through \(R=\Sigma_rX\Sigma_r^{-1}\), which is LoRA-XS's image. For injective \(W_0\), \(W_0S\) determines \(S\), so meeting with exact full-block Cayley OFT keeps exactly the \(S(T)\) with \(T\in SO(r)\) and no eigenvalue \(-1\), which is the Cayley image.

(b) Every \(S\in SO(V)\) has \(\operatorname{rank}(S-I)\le r\), which gives “\(\supseteq\)”. Conversely, if \(W_0S-W_0\) has rank at most \(r\) and \(W_0\) is injective, then \(\operatorname{rank}(S-I)\le r\). The fixed space \(F=\ker(S-I)\) has dimension at least \(n-r\), and \(F^\perp\) is invariant. Any \(r\)-dimensional \(V\supseteq F^\perp\) works: \(S\) is the identity on \(V^\perp\subseteq F\) and has determinant \(1\) on \(V\).

(c) \(D\rho_0(E)=W_0P_r^\top Q'(0)(E)P_r\), with \(Q'(0)\) invertible. If this vanishes, then \(W_0P_r^\top Q'(0)(E)=0\) because \(P_r\) has full row rank, and \(E=0\) because \(W_0P_r^\top\) has full column rank. So \(\dim S_1=\lvert M\rvert\ge d(M)\ge\dim S_1\). A smooth pointed arrow would give \(S_1(\mathrm{LOFT})\subseteq S_1(\mathrm{HRA})\) (Observation I.6), and by Theorem II.7(b) and injectivity this asks every \(\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(V)\) to satisfy \(P_{U^\perp}\Omega P_{U^\perp}=0\). Since \(\dim(V\cap U^\perp)\ge r-r/2\ge2\), any two orthonormal \(a,b\in V\cap U^\perp\) give \(a\wedge b\) violating it.

(d) With \(Q'(0)=\mathrm{id}\), \(\tfrac{d}{dt}L\big(\rho(tE)\big)\big|_{0}=\langle G,W_0P_r^\top EP_r\rangle=\langle P_rW_0^\top GP_r^\top,E\rangle=\langle P_r\operatorname{skew}(W_0^\top G)P_r^\top,E\rangle\), since \(E\) is skew. The bound is a Ky Fan inequality for the skew matrix \(\operatorname{skew}(W_0^\top G)\) compressed to \(V\). ∎

Numerical check (\(n=8\), \(m=10\), \(r=2,4,6\)): the leaf identity, the union, the arrows into exact Cayley OFT and LoRA-XS, and \(\dim S_1=r(r-1)/2\) all hold to about \(10^{-15}\).

Their Table 1 writes full and block OFT, GOFT, BOFT, HRA and PSOFT as choices of support, width, depth and transform in a multi-factor version of the same primitive. Orthogonal methods differ in which leaves they use and how they combine them. Block OFT, GOFT and BOFT multiply rotations of fixed coordinate subspaces; HRA moves its leaf while it trains; PSOFT takes the principal leaf and adds two tori; LOFT takes one leaf, chosen before training from \(W_0\) or from the gradient at \(\theta_0\). By part (d), SkewGrad is the orthogonal counterpart of the alignment mechanism of Proposition II.3, and GradSVD transplants the gradient-based frames of LoRA-GA. With the leaf frozen, merge-and-restart gains nothing. With finitely many supports revisited across restarts, the reach is the orbit of the connected group whose Lie algebra the \(\mathfrak{so}(V_k)\) generate (Theorem V.3(b)); numerically, generic supports with \(r\ge2\) generate all of \(SO(n)\) once \(\lceil n/r\rceil\) of them are used. The principal support does not move when it is re-selected after a merge, and data-chosen supports have to be checked case by case.

DoRA is a torus saturation of LoRA

DoRA normalises each row of LoRA's matrix \(V=W_0+BA\) and multiplies row \(i\) by a learned magnitude \(\mu_i\). That is one row scaling, by \(\mu_i/\|v_i\|\), so every DoRA weight is a diagonal matrix times a LoRA weight, and conversely up to a repair for vanishing rows. We write \(\mathrm{Diag}_m\) for the monoid of diagonal matrices, \(\mathrm{Mon}_m=T_m\rtimes S_m\) for the monomial matrices, and \(N\) for row normalisation.

Proposition II.8 (DoRA is LoRA followed by an output scale, at the level of images). Let \(1\le r\le\min(m,n)\) and \(s\ne0\), and let DoRA's magnitude \(\mu\in\mathbb R^m\) be per output neuron, as in HF PEFT. Let \(W_0\) have no zero row, and let \(\mathcal U\) be the open set of matrices with no zero row. Then \[\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{DoRA}_r=\{\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,N(V):V\in(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})\cap\mathcal U,\ \mu\in\mathbb R^m\}=\mathrm{Diag}_m\cdot(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r}),\] which is exactly the image of “\(\mathrm{LoRA}_r\) followed by a trainable output scale \(\ell\in\mathbb R^m\)”. With \(\mu\) restricted to \((\mathbb R^\times)^m\), or equivalently after intersecting with \(\mathcal U\), \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{DoRA}_r\cap\mathcal U=T_m\cdot(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})\cap\mathcal U\). Moreover:
  • \(d(\mathrm{DoRA}_r)\le\min\big(mn,\ r(m+n-r)+m\big)\). At a parameter point with every \(\mu_i\ne0\) and \(X=V-W_0\in\mathcal M_r\), with \(U=\mathrm{col}\,X\) and \(R=\mathrm{row}\,X\), the Jacobian rank is \(r(m+n-r)+m-\dim\ker\big(\delta\mapsto P_{U^\perp}\mathrm{diag}(\delta)W_0P_{R^\perp}\big)\); where some \(\mu_i=0\) it is smaller when \(r\ge2\), and for \(r=1\) when some row \(w_{0,i}\) with \(\mu_i=0\) lies outside \(R\). For generic \(W_0\), \(d=\min\big(mn,\ r(m+n-r)+m\big)\) Num, which equals \(r(m+n-r)+m\) iff \((m-r)(n-r)\ge m\), as for all practical shapes. For special \(W_0\) it is smaller, for instance \(r(m+n-r)+m-r\) for a rank-one \(W_0\) without zero rows when \(r\lt m\) and \(r\le n-2\) Num.
  • The image covariance group (Definition II.14) contains \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times GL_n\).
  • At \(B=0\), in HF's magnitude coordinates, \(\Delta W=\big(\mathrm{diag}(\mu_i/\|w_{0,i}\|)-I\big)W_0\), which can have rank up to \(\operatorname{rank}W_0\), so DoRA escapes LoRA's rank bound.
(expand)

\(\mathrm{diag}(\mu)N(V)=\mathrm{diag}(\mu_i/\|v_i\|)\,V\in\mathrm{Diag}_m\cdot V\). Conversely, take \(\mathrm{diag}(\ell)V\) with \(V=W_0+X\), \(\operatorname{rank}X\le r\), and let \(Z\) be the set of zero rows of \(V\). Put \(V'=W_0+(I-\varepsilon\sum_{i\in Z}E_{ii})X\). Its rows outside \(Z\) are those of \(V\), its rows in \(Z\) are \(\varepsilon w_{0,i}\ne0\), and its update still has rank \(\le r\). With \(\ell'_i=0\) on \(Z\) and \(\ell'_i=\ell_i\) elsewhere, \(\mathrm{diag}(\ell')V'=\mathrm{diag}(\ell)V\) and \(V'\in\mathcal U\). For the \(T_m\) statement, \(DV\in\mathcal U\) with \(D\) invertible forces \(V\in\mathcal U\).

Dimension. \(\rho_{\rm DoRA}=\rho_{\rm LoRA+scale}\circ\varphi\) with \(\varphi(\mu,B,A)=(\mu_i/\|v_i\|,B,A)\), a diffeomorphism of DoRA's domain, so ranks may be computed for \((D,X)\mapsto D(W_0+X)\). For invertible \(D\) the differential at \((D,X)\) is \(D\) times the one at \((I,X)\) after \(\delta\mapsto D^{-1}\delta\). Its image at \((I,X)\) is \(\{\mathrm{diag}(\delta)V\}+T_X\mathcal M_r\); the first summand has dimension \(m\) since \(V\in\mathcal U\), and \(\mathrm{diag}(\delta)V\in T_X\mathcal M_r\) iff \(P_{U^\perp}\mathrm{diag}(\delta)VP_{R^\perp}=0\), where \(VP_{R^\perp}=W_0P_{R^\perp}\). A zero \(\mu_i\) kills row \(i\) of the LoRA directions, leaving only \(\delta_iv_i\) there, where an invertible magnitude also gives every \(e_iy^\top\) with \(y\in R\). Since \(v_i-w_{0,i}\in R\), the rank falls below the formula by at least \(\dim\mathrm{span}(w_{0,i},R)-1\) for each such \(i\), which is positive when \(r\ge2\) or \(w_{0,i}\notin R\). For the generic value, a proof sketch: with bases \(E\) of \(U^\perp\) and \(F\) of \(R^\perp\) the kernel map is \(\delta\mapsto\sum_i\delta_i(E^\top e_i)\otimes(F^\top W_0^\top e_i)\), and \(m\) generic points of the Segre cone in \(\mathbb R^{m-r}\otimes\mathbb R^{n-r}\) span \(\min(m,(m-r)(n-r))\) dimensions.

Covariance. A monomial \(g\) normalises \(\mathrm{Diag}_m\), and \(g\mathcal M_{\le r}h^{-1}=\mathcal M_{\le r}\), so \(g\,\mathrm{Diag}_m(W_0+\mathcal M_{\le r})h^{-1}=\mathrm{Diag}_m(gW_0h^{-1}+\mathcal M_{\le r})\). ∎

Numerical check: Jacobian rank \(9=mn\) at \((m,n,r)=(3,3,2)\), where \(r(m+n-r)+m\) gives \(11\); \(36\) at \((8,8,2)\), matching; \(24\) for a rank-one \(W_0\) at \((6,6,2)\). With one \(\mu_i=0\) at \((8,8,2)\) the rank drops to \(34\).

More: which weight, which norm

Images refer to the merged weight; in training mode with LoRA dropout, HF's DoRA forward is not of the form \(Wx\). The DoRA paper's notation normalises “column-wise”, while both its official code and HF PEFT normalise each output row.

At the level of images, DoRA's “magnitude–direction decoupling” is LoRA plus \(m\) free output gains; everything else happens in training. Under gradient flow the exact gradient of the normalised parametrisation is a change of metric, while the detached backward of the DoRA paper (Sec. 4.3) and HF PEFT (weight_norm.detach()) is a pseudo-gradient that no metric describes. Under Adam the coordinates matter too, as \(\mu_i\) starts at \(\|w_{0,i}\|\) and \(\ell_i\) at \(1\). Prediction X.2 (Conjecture D) uses the equal images to set up a controlled comparison.

The covariance group \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times GL_n\) decides where Observation II.15 applies. One orthogonal rotation of the residual stream of a gain-fused pre-norm model, as in SliceGPT, acts on the input side of \(q\), \(k\), \(v\), gate and up, where DoRA is \(GL_n\)-covariant, so DoRA on those boxes reaches the same functions after the rotation as before; on \(o\) and down the sufficient condition fails. Training outcomes are a separate question.

The torus ladder

Write a weight as \(W_0\odot(J+Z)\), with \(J\) the all-ones matrix. Output scalings use \(Z=(\ell-\mathbf 1)\mathbf 1^\top\), of rank one; two-sided scalings use \(ab^\top-J\), of rank at most two; HiRA allows any \(Z=BA\) of rank at most \(r\). These choices form a ladder of images, and no rung can fill a zero of \(W_0\).

Proposition II.9 (the torus ladder).
  1. HiRA. \(\rho(B,A)=W_0\odot(J+BA)\); on linear and convolutional layers HF PEFT has \(B_0=0\) and no scale factor. For every \(W_0\), \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HiRA}_r=W_0+D_{W_0}(\mathcal M_{\le r})\) with \(D_{W_0}:X\mapsto W_0\odot X\). If \(W_0\) has no zero entries, \(D_{W_0}\in GL(\Theta)\), so in the slice at \(\theta_0\) HiRA is LoRA transported by a diagonal automorphism, with the same gauge and the same \(d=r(m+n-r)\) for \(r\le\min(m,n)\). Always \(\operatorname{rank}\Delta W\le r\cdot\operatorname{rank}W_0\). The transport depends on \(\theta_0\) (Proposition II.13).
  2. Scalings. Output scalings \(\mathrm{diag}(\ell)W_0=W_0\odot(J+(\ell-\mathbf 1)\mathbf 1^\top)\) and input scalings \(W_0\mathrm{diag}(\ell)=W_0\odot(J+\mathbf 1(\ell-\mathbf 1)^\top)\) have images in \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HiRA}_1\). Output-side examples are (IA)³ on keys and values, SSF's post-linear scale and DoRA's magnitude. Input-side examples are (IA)³'s feed-forward vector, which scales the input of \(W_{\rm down}\), and a LayerNorm gain folded into the next linear map. The fold is exact when the norm's output feeds only linear maps, and it then ties the gain across them (Q, K and V, or gate and up). Two-sided scalings \(\mathrm{diag}(a)W_0\mathrm{diag}(b)=W_0\odot(ab^\top)\) lie in \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HiRA}_2\).
  3. Orbit dimension. The two-sided torus orbit \(\{\mathrm{diag}(a)W_0\mathrm{diag}(b)\}\) has dimension \(m+n-c(W_0)\), where \(c(W_0)\) is the number of connected components of the bipartite support graph of \(W_0\), an isolated vertex counting as its own component.
  4. RoAd. \(\mathrm{RoAd}_1\) (the paper's main variant, HF road_1) is \(W=RW_0\) with \(R\) block-diagonal in scaled rotations \(\alpha_i\mathrm{Rot}(\theta_i)\) of output pairs, the left action of \(CO^+(2)^{m/2}\cong(\mathbb C^\times)^{m/2}\), or (IA)³ over \(\mathbb C\). It multiplies the input Gram matrix of each output pair by \(\alpha_i^2\), and since \(\alpha_i\) may be \(0\) its image is the orbit closure, a real-linear space of dimension \(m\) when no output-pair block of \(W_0\) is zero. \(\mathrm{RoAd}_2\) and \(\mathrm{RoAd}_4\) let each block range over the monoid \(M_2(\mathbb R)\), with group part \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\); their linear image \(\{\mathrm{blockdiag}(R_i)W_0\}\) has dimension \(2m\) when every pair block has rank \(2\), the Gram statement and “over \(\mathbb C\)” fail, and \(\mathrm{RoAd}_4\) has a positive-dimensional gauge.
  5. Zeros and covariance. Every \(W\) reachable by HiRA, (IA)³ or SSF vanishes wherever \(W_0\) does, \(Z(W_0)\subseteq Z(W)\), and this persists under merging. New zeros can appear (\(\ell_i=0\), or \((J+BA)_{ij}=0\)), so the zero pattern itself is not invariant. The image covariance group of output-scaled boxes ((IA)³ on \(k,v\); post-linear SSF) is \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times GL_n\), and that of input-scaled boxes ((IA)³ on \(W_{\rm down}\)) is \(GL_m\times\mathrm{Mon}_n\). HiRA's is exactly \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times\mathrm{Mon}_n\).
(expand)

(a) \(W_0\odot(J+BA)=W_0+W_0\odot BA\). \(D_{W_0}\) is diagonal in the standard basis of \(\Theta\), and invertible iff no entry of \(W_0\) vanishes; pushforward along a linear isomorphism preserves dimension and gauge. For the rank, \(W_0\odot(BA)=\sum_k\mathrm{diag}(b_k)W_0\mathrm{diag}(a_k)\), summed over the \(r\) columns \(b_k\) of \(B\) and rows \(a_k\) of \(A\), which has rank \(\le r\operatorname{rank}W_0\).

(b) Direct computation, using \(ab^\top-J=[a,-\mathbf 1][b,\mathbf 1]^\top\in\mathcal M_{\le2}\).

(c) The tangent map at \((\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1)\) is \((\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\big((\alpha_i+\beta_j)(W_0)_{ij}\big)\). Its kernel requires \(\alpha_i+\beta_j=0\) along every support edge, so it is one-dimensional on each connected component. Torus orbits are homogeneous, so the dimension is the same at every point of the orbit.

(d) A \(2\times n\) real block \(X\) is a complex row in \(\mathbb C^n\), and \(\alpha\mathrm{Rot}(\theta)\) acts on it as multiplication by \(\alpha e^{i\theta}\). So the image for one pair is \(\mathbb C\cdot X\), of real dimension \(2\) iff \(X\ne0\), and \((\alpha\mathrm{Rot}X)^\top(\alpha\mathrm{Rot}X)=\alpha^2X^\top X\). In \(\mathrm{RoAd}_2\) each row of a block is an arbitrary vector of \(\mathbb R^2\) written in polar form.

(e) Hadamard multiplication and row or column scaling preserve the zeros of \(W_0\).

(IA)³ and SSF. A monomial \(g\) normalises the diagonal monoid, so \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times GL_n\) lies in the covariance group of output scalings. Conversely, take \(W_0=e_jc^\top\). Then \(\mathrm{Im}\,M(gW_0h^{-1})=\mathrm{span}\{e_i:i\in\mathrm{supp}(ge_j)\}\,c^\top h^{-1}\), while \(g\,\mathrm{Im}\,M(W_0)\,h^{-1}=\mathbb R\,(ge_j)c^\top h^{-1}\). Equality forces \(\lvert\mathrm{supp}(ge_j)\rvert=1\) for every \(j\), so \(g\) is monomial. This works for all \(m,n\). Input-side scalings are the transpose.

HiRA. Monomial pairs commute with Hadamard products up to relabelling, so \(\mathrm{Mon}_m\times\mathrm{Mon}_n\) lies in the covariance group. Conversely, take \(W_0=E_{ij}\). Then \(\mathrm{Im}=\mathbb R\,E_{ij}\), so covariance under \((g,h)\) forces \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{HiRA}_r(uv^\top)=\mathbb R\,uv^\top\) with \(u=ge_i\) and \(v=h^{-\top}e_j\). But that image contains \(uv^\top+(u\odot p)(v\odot q)^\top\) for all \(p,q\), which leaves the line \(\mathbb R\,uv^\top\) unless \(\lvert\mathrm{supp}\,u\rvert=\lvert\mathrm{supp}\,v\rvert=1\). So every \(ge_i\) and every \(h^{-\top}e_j\) has a single nonzero entry, and \(g\) and \(h\) are monomial. ∎

Numerical check: \(\mathrm{RoAd}_1\) orbit dimension \(8\) and \(\mathrm{RoAd}_2\) dimension \(16\) at \(m=8\).

More: pointings, LayerNorm folds and HF PEFT details

As a morphism, \(h(\ell)=(\ell-\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1^\top)\) lands in \(\mathrm{HiRA}_1\) pointed at \((0,\mathbf 1^\top)\), not at HiRA's random-\(A\) default. A LayerNorm gain folds exactly for the input norms of pre-norm blocks and a final norm before an untied head, but not for post-sublayer norms (Gemma-2 and 3) or QK-norms; LayerNorm biases, like SSF's shift, leave \(W\) alone. HF HiRA on embeddings starts at \(A_0=0\). HF RoAd pairs output \(j\) with \(j+g/2\) in groups of size \(g\) and also rotates the bias; nothing above depends on the pairing.
